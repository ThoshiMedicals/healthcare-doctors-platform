For new patients

Prescribing starts with a safe clinical review.

We welcome new patients, including people who already take regular medicines. A prescription is not automatic, however. Your doctor must be satisfied that prescribing is safe, clinically appropriate and supported by enough information about your health and current treatment.

Prescribing restrictions for new patients

  • Controlled, dependence-forming and high-risk medicines. These may not be prescribed at a first appointment without adequate verification, clinical assessment and any monitoring the doctor considers necessary.
  • Existing long-term medicines. Your doctor may need previous records, specialist letters, recent test results or confirmation from your usual prescriber or pharmacy before continuing a medicine.
  • Medicines requiring ongoing monitoring. A consultation, examination, pathology, other testing or specialist review may be required before a prescription can be issued.
  • Requests that are not clinically appropriate. A doctor may decline, defer or recommend a different treatment pathway when prescribing would not be safe or appropriate.

How to prepare for your appointment

  1. Bring an up-to-date medication list and, if useful, the medicine packaging.
  2. Bring relevant specialist letters, recent results or a health summary if you have them.
  3. Tell reception if the appointment relates to a medicine that may require a longer review.
  4. Book before your medicine supply runs out so there is time to review records or arrange follow-up if needed.
Clinical judgement comes first.

Reception can help with appointment arrangements, but only a qualified prescriber can decide whether a medicine should be prescribed, continued, changed or stopped.

Patient questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Will I automatically receive my regular medicines at my first appointment?

No. Prescribing is a clinical decision. Your doctor may need to confirm your medical history, current medicines, monitoring and previous records before deciding whether a prescription is appropriate.

What if I take a controlled or dependence-forming medicine?

New patients should not assume controlled, dependence-forming or other high-risk medicines will be prescribed at a first visit. The doctor may require records, additional assessment, monitoring or advice from your usual prescriber or specialist.

What should I bring if I need a medication review?

Bring an up-to-date medication list, medicine packaging if useful, relevant specialist letters and details of your usual prescriber or pharmacy.

What if I am running out of medicine?

Book early rather than waiting until your supply has run out. If you are concerned about missing a medicine or possible withdrawal, contact reception for advice about the most appropriate care pathway. For a medical emergency, call 000.
