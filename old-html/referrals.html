Specialist referrals

Our current policy requires a consultation with a doctor to obtain a referral. A referral summarises the clinical reason for specialist review and provides information that helps the specialist understand the question being asked.

How long a referral lasts

Most GP referrals to a specialist are valid for 12 months from the specialist’s first consultation with you, unless the referral states a different period. A GP can also issue a longer or indefinite referral when ongoing specialist care is clinically appropriate.

A new referral may still be needed for a new or unrelated condition.

Referral renewals

Do not wait until the day of a specialist appointment to discover that a referral has expired. If your specialist asks for a new referral, book with your GP in enough time for your care to be reviewed and the referral prepared.

Why a valid referral matters

A valid referral is generally required for Medicare to pay a specialist benefit. Without one, you may not receive the Medicare rebate for the specialist consultation.

Referral information — healthdirect

Appointment length

An expired referral renewal may be suitable for a brief appointment, while a new referral generally requires a standard consultation. If the referral relates to a new or complex problem, a longer consultation may be more appropriate.

Referral questions

Why might a referral request need a consultation?

A doctor may need to understand the clinical reason for referral, review relevant information and decide what type of referral is appropriate.
