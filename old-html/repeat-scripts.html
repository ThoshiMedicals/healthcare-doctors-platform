Renewing a prescription

Our current policy requires a consultation with a doctor for further prescriptions. This allows the prescriber to confirm that the medicine remains appropriate and that any monitoring or follow-up is up to date.

Some repeat prescriptions for regular medication may be suitable for a brief consultation, while other prescription requests may require a standard or longer appointment.

Electronic prescriptions

An electronic prescription is a digital alternative to a paper prescription. If your prescriber offers an eScript and it is appropriate for your medicine, a secure token can be sent by SMS or email and presented to a pharmacy for dispensing.

If a prescription has repeats, the pharmacy can issue a new token after each dispensing. If you lose an original token, contact the prescriber; for a repeat token, contact the pharmacy that issued it.

Electronic prescriptions — Australian Digital Health Agency

Medication safety

A prescription may not be renewed automatically. Your doctor may need to review your symptoms, dose, side effects, interactions, monitoring tests or the length of time you have been taking the medicine before prescribing again.

Choosing the right appointment

A repeat prescription for regular medication may be suitable for a brief appointment in some circumstances. A medication request that needs discussion may require a standard or longer consultation. Your doctor will advise if more time is needed for clinical review.

Prescription questions

Do I need a consultation for a repeat prescription?

Our policy requires a consultation with a doctor for further prescriptions so the prescriber can confirm that the medicine remains appropriate.

Can a repeat prescription be a brief appointment?

Some repeat prescriptions for regular medication may be suitable for a brief consultation, while other requests may require a standard or longer review.
