About this topic

Menopause symptoms, treatment discussions and emotional wellbeing during midlife.

These patient guides are for general information. If symptoms are severe or rapidly worsening, seek appropriate urgent medical advice.

How to use this hub

Education pathway

  • What this covers — Perimenopause and menopause symptoms, midlife health review, and treatment discussions including hormone therapy options.
  • When to seek care — Book when hot flushes, sleep change, mood change, bleeding pattern change, or midlife health questions affect daily life.
  • What a GP can do — A GP can clarify whether symptoms fit the menopause transition, discuss options, and arrange tests or referral when needed.
  • What to bring — Symptom timeline, bleeding history, medicines list, and questions about treatment preferences.
  • Trusted information — Use trusted Australian menopause and women's health resources alongside this hub.

Educational overview

Understanding menopause care

A menopause discussion can help you recognise life-stage changes, talk about symptoms and concerns, review overall health and plan follow-up support.

Care is tailored to the individual. This overview does not describe a specific treatment plan.

Patient questions

When should I book a menopause review?

Book if symptoms affect sleep, mood, work, relationships or daily comfort, or if you want to discuss treatment options, bleeding changes or long-term preventive health. Educational reading starts with Understanding Menopause.

Is perimenopause different from menopause?

Understanding Perimenopause explains the transition stage. A GP can help place your symptoms in context.

Can mental health be part of menopause care?

Yes. Mood, anxiety and sleep changes can be discussed alongside physical symptoms. See Mental health and menopause, and our Mental Health service if you need that pathway.

How does menopause care connect with women’s health services?

Book through Women’s Health. Cervical screening and preventive checks may still be relevant — ask your GP what applies to you.

Patient questions

Frequently asked questions

When are menopause-related symptoms urgent?

Seek urgent care for very heavy bleeding, fainting, severe chest pain, or sudden neurological symptoms. Call 000 for emergencies.

When should I book a GP about menopause?

Book when hot flushes, sleep disturbance, mood change, bleeding change, or midlife health questions affect daily life.

When do I need a longer menopause appointment?

Ask for a longer appointment if you want a full symptom review, treatment discussion, or several midlife health topics covered together.

What should I bring to a menopause visit?

Bring a symptom timeline, bleeding history, medicines list, and questions about treatment preferences.

Need support?

Discuss your concern with a GP.
