Understanding Women's Health

Women's healthcare changes across life stages and can include prevention, screening, reproductive health, pregnancy-related care, hormonal changes and mental wellbeing. A GP can help place each concern in the context of your medical history and preferences.

How to use this hub

Education pathway

  • What this covers — Prevention, screening, reproductive health, pregnancy-related care, hormonal change and midlife wellbeing in general practice.
  • When to seek care — Book for new or changing symptoms, overdue screening, contraception or menopause questions, or when several women's health concerns need time in one visit.
  • What a GP can do — A GP can assess, arrange screening or referral, discuss options, and coordinate ongoing care.
  • What to bring — Medicines list, prior results or letters, menstrual or symptom notes, and your main questions.
  • Trusted information — Prefer national guidance and the trusted links listed on related patient guides.

Educational overview

Women's health across life stages

Women's healthcare needs can change across life stages. Discussions may cover reproductive health, screening, pregnancy-related care, menopause and midlife health, and ongoing preventive care.

What is discussed depends on individual needs and circumstances.

Patient questions

Frequently asked questions

When is women's health an urgent problem?

Seek urgent or emergency care for severe pain, heavy uncontrolled bleeding, sudden severe symptoms in pregnancy, or any life-threatening concern. Call Triple Zero (000) when needed.

When should I book a GP for women's health?

Book for overdue screening, contraception or menopause questions, new pelvic or breast symptoms, pregnancy planning, or when several concerns need a structured visit.

When should I ask for a longer appointment?

Ask reception for a longer appointment if you have multiple issues, procedure planning, complex history, or menopause or pregnancy-related discussions.

What should I bring?

Bring a medicines list, prior results or letters, symptom notes, and the questions that matter most to you.
