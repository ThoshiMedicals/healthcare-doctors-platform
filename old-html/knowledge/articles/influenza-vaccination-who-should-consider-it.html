Introduction

Influenza is a viral respiratory infection that can be more severe than the common cold. Vaccination is an important way to reduce the risk of influenza illness and complications. Australian recommendations and available formulations are reviewed each season.

Why This Topic Matters

The Australian Immunisation Handbook recommends influenza vaccination each year for everyone aged six months and older. National Immunisation Program funding is available for certain eligible groups; the vaccine and dose schedule depend on age, medical history and current advice.

Signs, Symptoms or Situations Relevant to Patients

The following situations are worth recognising. They do not establish a diagnosis by themselves:

  • You are pregnant, aged 65 or older, caring for a young child, or have a chronic condition that increases influenza risk.
  • Your child is at least six months old and has not had the current season's recommended dose or doses.
  • You have not reviewed eligibility for funded vaccination or the available seasonal formulation.

When Should I Seek Medical Advice?

Arrange a vaccination discussion before the main influenza season where practical, but vaccination can still be useful later. If you are acutely unwell, ask whether to defer. Seek urgent care for severe breathing difficulty, confusion or other concerning illness; a vaccine appointment is not treatment for an emergency.

How Can a GP Help?

A GP or appropriately qualified immunisation provider can review your age, pregnancy, risk conditions and vaccination history to advise on the right seasonal vaccine. Some first-time vaccinations in young children or certain higher-risk groups require extra doses under current guidance. Confirm availability with the clinic. Patients from Woolloongabba and nearby Brisbane inner-south communities can discuss the most appropriate next step with the practice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does everyone need a flu vaccination every year?

Annual influenza vaccination is recommended for people aged six months and older; the suitable product and funding vary.

Is the influenza vaccination free for everyone?

No. The National Immunisation Program funds vaccines for eligible groups; charges and consultation arrangements should be checked.

Can I have a flu vaccine when pregnant?

Influenza vaccination is recommended during pregnancy. Ask your clinician about the appropriate non-live vaccine.
