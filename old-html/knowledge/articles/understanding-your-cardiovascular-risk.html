Introduction

Your cardiovascular risk is about more than one blood pressure or cholesterol reading. A GP can bring several health factors together to help you understand your future risk.

This guide is general information for patients in Woolloongabba and neighbouring inner-south Brisbane communities. It does not replace an individual clinical assessment.

What Is It?

Cardiovascular disease affects the heart and blood vessels, including conditions that may lead to a heart attack or stroke. A cardiovascular risk assessment estimates the likelihood of such an event over a defined period, usually five years in the Australian primary-care setting.

A GP may use the Australian cardiovascular disease risk calculator (Aus CVD Risk Calculator) for people who meet its criteria. The calculation combines factors including age, smoking, diabetes, blood pressure and blood cholesterol. It is an estimate, not a prediction of what will happen to a particular person.

Should I Be Concerned?

Heart and blood-vessel disease can develop before symptoms appear. A family history of early heart disease, diabetes, smoking, high blood pressure or high cholesterol is a reason to ask whether a preventive check is appropriate.

If you already have cardiovascular disease, your GP will manage your established condition rather than treat a calculator result as your complete care plan. Some people need assessment outside the usual age ranges because of particular conditions or risk factors.

Common Symptoms and Situations

Cardiovascular risk itself usually does not cause symptoms. This is why a routine review can be worthwhile even when you feel well.

Arrange a medical assessment for new breathlessness with activity, reduced exercise tolerance, palpitations or recurring chest discomfort. These symptoms need individual evaluation and should not be dismissed because a previous risk score was low.

Risk factors that may be identified during a health review include elevated blood pressure, changes in cholesterol, diabetes, tobacco exposure and some family-history patterns.

When Should I Seek Medical Advice?

Call Triple Zero (000) for suspected heart attack symptoms such as persistent chest pressure or pain, pain spreading to an arm or jaw, severe breathlessness, collapse, or stroke warning signs including facial droop, arm weakness or speech problems. Do not wait for a routine booking.

For a non-emergency preventive discussion, book a GP appointment if you are in an age or risk group that may benefit from assessment, have several risk factors, or do not understand previous test results.

Urgent symptoms: If someone is in immediate danger or has life-threatening symptoms, call 000. Do not use online booking for emergency care.

How Can a GP Help?

Your GP can explain why one factor may matter more in your circumstances and, where appropriate, use the Aus CVD Risk Calculator. Together you can discuss achievable changes to smoking, physical activity, food choices, sleep, blood pressure and cholesterol.

Some patients benefit from medicines, tests or additional support; others may focus first on monitoring and lifestyle changes. Your plan should account for health history, preferences, side effects, costs and follow-up needs.

What Happens at the Appointment?

Expect questions about family history, medicines, smoking or vaping, movement, health conditions and any symptoms. Your GP may measure blood pressure and review recent cholesterol or blood glucose results, ordering tests only when useful.

If the calculator applies, your GP can discuss a five-year percentage in plain language, explain uncertainty and agree on practical next steps. Bring home blood pressure readings, prior results and your medicine list if available. Eligibility for specific Medicare-funded checks should be confirmed with the practice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is cardiovascular risk the same as my cholesterol level?

No. Cholesterol is one risk factor. Cardiovascular risk combines several factors to estimate the chance of a heart attack or stroke over time.

Does a low score mean I cannot have a heart attack?

No. A low estimate means lower predicted risk, not zero risk. New or concerning symptoms always need assessment regardless of a previous score.

Do I need an ECG as part of every risk check?

Not necessarily. A preventive cardiovascular risk review is not the same as testing for acute heart symptoms. Your GP can explain which investigations are appropriate.

Can I lower my cardiovascular risk?

Many risk factors can be improved through smoking cessation, blood pressure or cholesterol treatment, movement and other changes. The best steps depend on your medical history.
