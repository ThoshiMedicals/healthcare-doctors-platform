Introduction

Mood changes during midlife can be confusing, particularly when they happen alongside disrupted sleep and physical menopausal symptoms. The connection deserves assessment without assuming hormones explain everything.

What Is It?

Hormonal changes during perimenopause may coincide with anxiety, irritability, low mood, reduced concentration or sleep problems. Work, caregiving, previous mental health conditions and other medical factors can also influence how a person feels.

  • Mood changes with hot flushes
  • Difficulty sleeping and concentrating
  • Persistent anxiety, irritability or low mood

Should I Be Concerned?

Book an appointment if mood or anxiety symptoms persist, interfere with relationships or work, or affect your ability to cope. Share sleep changes, menstrual history, medicines and whether symptoms occur alongside hot flushes or night sweats.

When Should I Seek Medical Advice?

If you are at immediate risk of harming yourself or another person, call 000 or attend an emergency department. New severe confusion, chest pain or major physical symptoms must not be attributed to menopause alone.

How Can a GP Help?

A GP can review mental and physical health together, discuss talking therapies, sleep support, non-hormonal treatment and whether MHT may help menopausal symptoms. Follow-up is important to assess what is improving and what still needs care.

Notes about menstrual changes, sleep, hot flushes and how symptoms affect your day can help guide a personalised discussion. If unsure which appointment to choose, call reception on (07) 3217 3344.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can menopause cause anxiety?

Hormonal changes and sleep disruption may contribute, but anxiety can have other causes that also deserve assessment.

Can I discuss mental health and menopause in one GP appointment?

Yes. Ask reception whether a longer appointment would allow enough time for a fuller discussion.
