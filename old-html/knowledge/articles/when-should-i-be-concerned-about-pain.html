Introduction

Pain is common and has many causes. A useful assessment considers where it is, when it started, how it is changing and whether it affects normal activities.

What Is It?

Pain may follow an injury, inflammation, muscle strain, nerve irritation or illness. The intensity of pain alone does not identify the cause. Sudden unexplained pain, pain with fever and pain affecting movement sometimes require prompt attention.

  • Pain changing rapidly
  • Pain with fever or new neurological symptoms
  • Persistent pain affecting daily life

Should I Be Concerned?

Arrange medical review for pain that is persistent, worsening, unexplained, recurrent or interfering with sleep, work or self-care. Tell your GP about injury, fever, numbness, weakness, medicines and any changes in bowel or bladder function.

When Should I Seek Medical Advice?

Call 000 for chest pressure or pain suggestive of a heart attack, severe sudden abdominal pain with collapse, or pain with stroke-like symptoms. Severe pain after major trauma or with sudden loss of limb function also needs emergency assessment.

Urgent symptoms: If this is a life-threatening emergency, call 000. Do not use the booking form for emergency care.

How Can a GP Help?

A GP can examine the affected area, discuss likely causes and plan activity, pain relief or investigations based on clinical findings. For longer-term pain, coordinated rehabilitation and follow-up may be more useful than repeated short-term treatment.

Consider noting when pain started, how it affects movement and sleep, and any treatments already tried. If unsure which appointment to choose, call reception on (07) 3217 3344.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does severe pain always mean serious damage?

Not always. Pain intensity and tissue injury do not always match. New severe pain still deserves assessment, especially with warning symptoms.

Can I keep exercising with pain?

Activity advice depends on the cause. Gentle movement is often helpful, but do not push through major injury or rapidly worsening pain.
