Introduction

Vaccination does not end in childhood. Adults may need routine, age-based, risk-based, occupational, pregnancy-related or travel vaccines, as well as catch-up doses if earlier vaccination is incomplete.

Why This Topic Matters

Vaccination remains important in adulthood because protection and exposure change with age, pregnancy, work, travel and underlying illness. Recommendations and National Immunisation Program funding are not identical for everyone.

Signs, Symptoms or Situations to Recognise

Your GP may review influenza, COVID-19, tetanus-containing boosters, shingles or pneumococcal vaccination, depending on individual eligibility. Vaccine history should be checked rather than assuming a dose is needed.

  • You have an incomplete or uncertain vaccination record.
  • You are pregnant, immunocompromised or planning international travel.
  • You want to confirm funded vaccine eligibility before an appointment.

Adult vaccination depends on age, health and exposure risk

The National Immunisation Program Schedule and Australian Immunisation Handbook are updated as recommendations change. Eligibility for funded vaccines is not the same as whether a vaccine may be clinically recommended, so both recommendation and funding need to be checked.

People with chronic disease, immune conditions, pregnancy, particular occupations, planned travel or uncertain vaccination history may need an individual review.

  • Check your Australian Immunisation Register record
  • Discuss travel well before departure
  • Ask about age- and risk-based vaccines
  • Bring overseas records if you have them

When Should I Seek Medical Advice?

Book a vaccination review if you are unsure what is due, are planning travel, have a new medical risk factor or have an incomplete or uncertain vaccination history.

Urgent care: If symptoms are severe, rapidly worsening or you believe there is a life-threatening emergency, call 000.

How Can a GP Help?

A GP can review your history, identify recommended vaccines, check contraindications and precautions, discuss funding or private costs and document administered vaccines appropriately.

Preventive care can be built into routine GP visits for patients across Woolloongabba and Brisbane's inner south rather than treated as a one-off checklist.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are adult vaccines all free?

No. Some are funded for eligible groups under national or state programs, while others may involve a private cost.

What if I cannot find my old vaccination records?

A GP can review the available record and current guidance and discuss whether catch-up vaccination is appropriate.

How early should I ask about travel vaccines?

Ideally well before travel, because some vaccines require more than one dose or time for immunity to develop.
