Introduction

Blood pressure measures the force of blood against artery walls. Your reading has two numbers: systolic pressure when the heart contracts and diastolic pressure when it relaxes. One elevated reading does not always establish a diagnosis, because stress, activity and measurement technique can influence the result.

Why This Topic Matters

Persistently high blood pressure increases the risk of stroke, heart disease and kidney problems even when you feel well. Lower blood pressure can also matter when associated with dizziness or falls. Diagnosis and treatment use repeated, correctly measured readings and individual risk factors.

Signs, Symptoms or Situations Relevant to Patients

The following situations are worth recognising. They do not establish a diagnosis by themselves:

  • Repeated higher-than-expected readings at home, pharmacy or the clinic.
  • Dizziness or feeling faint when standing, particularly after medicine changes.
  • Family history of early cardiovascular disease, diabetes or kidney disease that raises the importance of risk assessment.

When Should I Seek Medical Advice?

Book a GP appointment to review repeated elevated readings, possible medicine side effects or uncertainty about home measurements. Severe headache with neurological symptoms, chest pain, major breathlessness or suspected stroke needs emergency assistance: call 000.

How Can a GP Help?

A GP may repeat measurements, suggest home or ambulatory monitoring, review medicines and check heart, kidney or diabetes risk as appropriate. Treatment can include activity, salt reduction, limiting alcohol, medicines and follow-up. Your target and review timing depend on your broader health. Patients from Woolloongabba and nearby Brisbane inner-south communities can discuss the most appropriate next step with the practice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can high blood pressure cause no symptoms?

Yes. Many people with hypertension feel entirely well. That is why opportunistic checks can be useful.

Is a single high reading a diagnosis?

Usually not. Your GP may consider repeated measurements and home or ambulatory readings.

Should I stop my blood pressure tablets if a reading is normal?

Do not change prescribed medicines without discussing the results and any symptoms with your treating clinician.
