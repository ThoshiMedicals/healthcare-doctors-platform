Introduction

Weight and health are connected, but a number on the scale does not describe the whole person. A helpful plan focuses on wellbeing, sustainable habits and any medical issues affecting weight.

What Is It?

Body weight can be influenced by genetics, medicines, sleep, hormones, living circumstances, activity and food access. Evidence-based care avoids blame and extreme dieting, and considers blood pressure, blood glucose, cholesterol, mobility and quality of life.

  • Weight affecting blood pressure or diabetes
  • Unexplained weight change
  • Questions about evidence-based support

Should I Be Concerned?

Book a conversation if weight changes unexpectedly, affects health or movement, or if repeated attempts to change weight have been distressing. Rapid unintentional weight loss, persistent fatigue or other symptoms deserve medical review rather than a diet plan alone.

When Should I Seek Medical Advice?

Sudden weight changes with severe illness, confusion, dehydration or other emergency symptoms require urgent assessment. Weight management appointments are not a substitute for treatment of acute illness or disordered eating symptoms that threaten safety.

How Can a GP Help?

A GP can assess relevant health risks and possible medical contributors, review medicines and work with you on realistic goals. Options may include dietitian or exercise-physiology support, sleep assessment or medicines when clinically indicated.

You can discuss your own health priorities, medicines, activity limits and what changes feel realistic without judgement. If unsure which appointment to choose, call reception on (07) 3217 3344.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there one best diet for everyone?

No. A sustainable approach that meets nutritional needs and fits a person’s circumstances is preferable to rigid fad diets.

Will my GP prescribe weight-loss medicines?

Medicines may be considered for some patients after assessment of indications, benefits, risks and alternatives; they are not suitable for everyone.
