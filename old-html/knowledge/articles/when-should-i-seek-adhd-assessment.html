Introduction

Many people experience distraction or restlessness. An assessment may be helpful when these difficulties have been persistent, have a developmental history and are significantly affecting everyday life.

What Is It?

ADHD symptoms may look different in children and adults. Examples include difficulties organising tasks, completing work, waiting, controlling impulses or managing competing demands. Sleep problems, anxiety and learning difficulties can overlap with ADHD symptoms.

  • Longstanding organisational difficulties
  • Symptoms affecting school, work or relationships
  • Uncertainty about ADHD versus other conditions

Should I Be Concerned?

Consider a GP appointment if difficulties affect school, employment, relationships or self-care across more than one setting, particularly where there is evidence they began in childhood. In children, feedback from family and educators may be useful.

When Should I Seek Medical Advice?

Immediate risks to safety, self-harm concerns or severe behavioural crises warrant urgent support; call 000 for immediate danger. Routine ADHD assessment is not a crisis service.

How Can a GP Help?

A GP can explore symptom history, medical and mental health considerations, and whether assessment through a paediatrician, psychiatrist, psychologist or other appropriately qualified professional is suitable. Referral choice and costs should be discussed.

Specific examples of difficulties at school, home or work and any earlier reports may help clarify referral needs. If unsure which appointment to choose, call reception on (07) 3217 3344.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can adults be assessed for ADHD?

Yes. Adult assessment considers current functioning and evidence of symptoms beginning in childhood.

Is difficulty concentrating always ADHD?

No. Sleep, mood, medicines, stress and health conditions can produce similar difficulties.
