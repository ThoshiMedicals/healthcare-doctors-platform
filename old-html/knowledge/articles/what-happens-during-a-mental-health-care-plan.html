Introduction

A mental health treatment plan is one way a GP can organise care when mental health symptoms need more structured support. The appointment is not simply paperwork: it is an opportunity to understand what you are experiencing and agree on an appropriate care pathway.

Why This Topic Matters

A mental health treatment plan is a clinical assessment and shared approach to support, not a form automatically provided to everyone. Your GP considers symptoms, functioning, risks, preferences and available therapies before deciding whether a plan is appropriate.

Signs, Symptoms or Situations to Recognise

You may be asked about mood, sleep, anxiety, social support, existing treatment and safety. You can bring notes or request extra time. Medicare eligibility and available subsidised allied mental health sessions depend on current rules and individual circumstances.

  • You would like to discuss psychological therapy options.
  • Symptoms interfere with relationships, employment or study.
  • You need to review an existing treatment plan or ask about follow-up.

A structured conversation about your health and support needs

Your GP may ask about symptoms, sleep, mood, anxiety, day-to-day functioning, physical health, medicines, alcohol or other substances, relationships, work and safety. Some questions can feel personal, but they help build a fuller picture.

If a treatment plan is clinically appropriate, the GP can discuss goals, treatment options, referrals and follow-up. Medicare arrangements and referral eligibility can change, so current entitlements should be confirmed rather than assumed from older information.

  • Allow enough appointment time for a meaningful assessment
  • Bring a medicines list and relevant previous mental health information if available
  • Think about the main changes you want help with
  • Ask what follow-up is recommended and how referrals will work

When Should I Seek Medical Advice?

You can speak with a GP whenever mental health concerns are persistent, distressing or affecting daily life. You do not need to wait for a crisis. For immediate danger or an inability to stay safe, call 000.

Urgent care: If symptoms are severe, rapidly worsening or you believe there is a life-threatening emergency, call 000.

How Can a GP Help?

A GP can assess your current needs, consider whether a mental health treatment plan is appropriate, arrange eligible referrals, discuss medicines when relevant and coordinate ongoing reviews.

Patients from Woolloongabba, East Brisbane and nearby inner-south communities can start with a GP when they are unsure what kind of mental health support may be appropriate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a mental health treatment plan automatic if I ask for one?

No. Your GP first assesses your needs and whether a plan is clinically appropriate.

Will I need to discuss personal information?

The GP may ask about symptoms, functioning, safety and other personal factors because these can affect care. You can ask why a question is relevant.

How many psychology visits does Medicare cover?

Entitlements and referral rules can change. Your GP and Services Australia can confirm current Medicare arrangements for your circumstances.
