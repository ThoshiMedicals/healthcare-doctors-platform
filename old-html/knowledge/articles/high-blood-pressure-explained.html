Introduction

High blood pressure often causes no symptoms. Understanding what the readings mean is more useful than waiting to feel unwell.

What Is It?

Blood pressure has two numbers: systolic pressure while the heart beats and diastolic pressure while it relaxes. Diagnosis usually relies on repeated accurate measurements, sometimes including home or 24-hour monitoring, rather than one reading during a stressful day.

  • Repeated elevated readings
  • High blood pressure with other health conditions
  • Questions about medication side effects

Should I Be Concerned?

See a GP if readings remain high, you have diabetes or kidney disease, or you have been advised to monitor pressure. Bring any home readings and details of medicines, including over-the-counter treatments.

When Should I Seek Medical Advice?

Call 000 if high blood pressure occurs with chest pain, severe breathlessness, confusion, new weakness, speech difficulty or sudden major visual change. Very high readings with severe symptoms need emergency assessment.

Urgent symptoms: If this is a life-threatening emergency, call 000. Do not use the booking form for emergency care.

How Can a GP Help?

A GP can check measurement technique, review cardiovascular risk and discuss medicines and practical approaches such as movement, lower-salt eating and limiting alcohol. Treatment targets depend on your overall health.

Bring home blood-pressure readings, recent test results and an up-to-date list of prescribed and over-the-counter medicines. If unsure which appointment to choose, call reception on (07) 3217 3344.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I feel high blood pressure?

Often not. Regular checks are important because high blood pressure may have no warning symptoms.

Is a single high reading a diagnosis?

Usually not. Clinicians consider repeated measurements and the setting in which they were taken.
