Introduction

Vomiting or diarrhoea raises a practical question: is this gastro, and do I need an appointment? This guide is for adults and for parents. It does not diagnose a cause and it does not describe treatment.

What Is It?

People use tummy bug and gastro for vomiting, diarrhoea, or both. Those phrases point to the same kind of concern. They are not a diagnosis.

  • Vomiting you are calling a tummy bug
  • Diarrhoea you are calling gastro
  • Vomiting and diarrhoea together

This guide is not the childhood-illness overview. Common Childhood Illnesses is a different topic.

Should I Be Concerned?

New vomiting or diarrhoea is an everyday reason a general practice visit can help, for an adult or for a child. This guide does not judge how serious a symptom is.

When Should I Seek Medical Advice?

Seek medical advice when you would otherwise guess the cause, or when the symptoms are worsening. Do not use this page to decide that they can wait.

Urgent care. If symptoms are severe, rapidly worsening, or you believe there is a life-threatening emergency, call 000. Do not use the booking form for emergency care.

How Can a GP Help?

A general practitioner can listen to what has changed and decide whether an examination or a follow-up plan is appropriate. That assessment happens at a general practice visit. This guide does not name a practitioner and it does not describe a procedure.

Ask reception for a general practice appointment and say what has changed. This page does not choose an appointment type. If you are unsure which appointment to book, call reception on (07) 3217 3344.

Adults and parents can both book a general practice visit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a stomach bug the same as gastroenteritis?

Stomach bug is everyday wording. Gastroenteritis is the clinical name people also meet. This page does not treat those names as a diagnosis.

Is a tummy bug food poisoning?

This page cannot say that. Food poisoning is a different question. Tummy bug and gastro remain everyday words, not a cause.
