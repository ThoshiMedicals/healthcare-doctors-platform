Introduction

There is no universal checklist of tests that every man needs every year. Good preventive care is based on age, symptoms, family history, lifestyle, previous results and existing medical conditions.

Why This Topic Matters

A preventive consultation can address cardiovascular risk, smoking, alcohol use, weight, activity, vaccinations and age-appropriate screening. The goal is to choose checks based on medical history rather than order every possible blood test.

Signs, Symptoms or Situations to Recognise

Consider a review if you have raised blood pressure, family history of early heart disease, changes in urination, persistent fatigue or concerns about sexual wellbeing. Some important risks, such as high blood pressure, cause no symptoms.

  • You have not reviewed blood pressure or cardiovascular risk recently.
  • You have new urinary symptoms or a concerning family history.
  • You want a practical plan for screening, vaccinations or lifestyle changes.

Start with risk, not a generic test panel

A preventive health review may include blood pressure, cardiovascular risk, diabetes risk, vaccination, bowel screening, skin health, mental wellbeing, smoking and alcohol use, weight and physical activity. Prostate discussions may be relevant depending on age, symptoms, family history and preferences.

The value of regular GP care is that these areas can be reviewed together. Tests can then be ordered for a clear reason rather than simply repeated by habit.

  • Know your blood pressure and cardiovascular risk
  • Participate in recommended national screening programs
  • Discuss family history of prostate, bowel, heart and other disease
  • Keep vaccinations up to date
  • Raise mental health, sleep, sexual health or urinary symptoms early

When Should I Seek Medical Advice?

Book a GP appointment if you are overdue for preventive care, have a strong family history, notice urinary or sexual health changes, or have new symptoms such as chest discomfort, unexplained weight loss or persistent fatigue.

Urgent care: If symptoms are severe, rapidly worsening or you believe there is a life-threatening emergency, call 000.

How Can a GP Help?

A GP can review your risk profile, identify recommended screening, discuss the benefits and limitations of tests and create a follow-up plan that fits your health history.

Patients in Woolloongabba, Kangaroo Point, Greenslopes and nearby communities can discuss men's health concerns with a GP in a confidential setting.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do men need an annual blood test?

Not automatically. Blood tests should be selected according to symptoms, age, risk factors and clinical need.

Should every man have a PSA test?

Prostate testing is a preference- and risk-sensitive decision. A GP can explain potential benefits, limitations and follow-up before testing.

Can a health check include mental health?

Yes. Mood, stress, sleep and substance use are important parts of overall preventive healthcare.
