Introduction

Australia has high ultraviolet exposure and a high burden of skin cancer. Skin awareness matters because many skin cancers are first noticed as a new spot or a change to an existing one. A skin check is an opportunity to assess concerning lesions and individual risk.

Why This Topic Matters

Australia has substantial ultraviolet exposure, and skin cancer is common. Skin examinations focus on concerning lesions and personal risk; a GP can advise whether regular checks are suitable for you.

Signs, Symptoms or Situations to Recognise

Watch for changing moles, new spots, sores that do not heal or lesions that bleed without explanation. Skin type, sunburn history and previous skin cancers influence the appropriate assessment plan.

  • A mole is changing in size, shape or colour.
  • A spot bleeds, crusts or repeatedly fails to heal.
  • You have previous skin cancer or substantial risk and need an individual monitoring plan.

Early attention to change is more useful than waiting for symptoms

Skin cancers do not all look the same. Changes in size, shape, colour or texture, a spot that looks different from others, a sore that does not heal, bleeding or persistent tenderness can all justify medical review.

There is no single skin-check interval that is right for everyone. Personal risk depends on factors such as previous skin cancer, sun exposure, skin type, number and type of moles, family history and immune status.

  • Become familiar with your own skin
  • Check areas that are hard to see as well as sun-exposed skin
  • Book promptly for a new or changing lesion
  • Discuss your personal risk rather than relying on a generic interval

When Should I Seek Medical Advice?

Book a medical assessment for a new, changing, bleeding, non-healing or otherwise concerning spot. Rapidly changing lesions or symptoms that concern you should not be delayed until a routine future check.

Urgent care: If symptoms are severe, rapidly worsening or you believe there is a life-threatening emergency, call 000.

How Can a GP Help?

A GP can examine the skin, use dermoscopy where available and appropriate, decide whether monitoring, biopsy, treatment or specialist referral is needed, and discuss a risk-based approach to future checks.

For patients living and working around Woolloongabba, South Brisbane and Greenslopes, sun protection and skin awareness are relevant throughout much of the year.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I have a skin check?

The appropriate interval depends on your individual risk and history. Ask your clinician what is reasonable for you.

Can skin cancer occur on areas that are not usually in the sun?

Yes. Although UV exposure is a major risk, skin cancers can appear in less exposed areas, so whole-skin awareness is important.

What if a mole looks different from my other moles?

A spot that looks or behaves differently from others is worth medical assessment, especially if it is new or changing.
