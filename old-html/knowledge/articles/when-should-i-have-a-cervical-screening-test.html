Introduction

Australia’s National Cervical Screening Program uses HPV testing to reduce the risk of cervical cancer. For most eligible women and people with a cervix aged 25 to 74, routine screening is recommended every five years, but individual circumstances can change what is appropriate.

Why This Topic Matters

Cervical screening looks for HPV associated with cervical cancer, rather than waiting for symptoms. Under the National Cervical Screening Program, most eligible people with a cervix aged 25–74 are invited every five years; individual follow-up can differ after an abnormal result.

Signs, Symptoms or Situations to Recognise

Routine screening is intended for people without symptoms. Bleeding after sex, bleeding between periods, bleeding after menopause or unusual persistent discharge warrants assessment even if your screening is up to date.

  • You are due for routine screening or unsure when your last test was.
  • You would like to discuss self-collection and clinician collection.
  • You have symptoms that need diagnostic assessment, not just routine screening.

Routine screening and symptom assessment are not the same thing

The Cervical Screening Test checks for human papillomavirus (HPV), which can cause cervical cell changes. Eligible participants can discuss clinician-collected and self-collected sampling options with their healthcare provider.

Routine screening is designed for people without symptoms. Symptoms such as unusual vaginal bleeding, bleeding after sex, persistent unusual discharge or pelvic symptoms should be discussed with a clinician even if your next routine screening test is not due.

  • Most eligible participants are invited from age 25 to 74
  • Routine interval is generally five years when previous results and circumstances allow
  • Self-collection is an option for eligible participants under the national program
  • Previous abnormal results, immune conditions or other clinical factors can change follow-up

When Should I Seek Medical Advice?

See a GP if you are unsure when you are due, have received an abnormal result, have symptoms such as unusual bleeding or discharge, or need help choosing a collection option. Significant bleeding, severe pain or rapidly worsening symptoms may need urgent assessment.

Urgent care: If symptoms are severe, rapidly worsening or you believe there is a life-threatening emergency, call 000.

How Can a GP Help?

A GP can confirm your screening history, explain current eligibility, arrange or perform screening, discuss self-collection, interpret results and coordinate follow-up or specialist referral if needed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Cervical Screening Test the same as the old Pap smear?

The current program primarily tests for HPV rather than looking first for abnormal cells, although people still commonly use the term Pap smear.

Can I collect my own sample?

Eligible participants can choose self-collection under the National Cervical Screening Program. Your healthcare provider can explain whether it is suitable for you.

Should I wait for my screening date if I have symptoms?

No. Symptoms need clinical assessment and should not be delayed until the next routine screening test.
