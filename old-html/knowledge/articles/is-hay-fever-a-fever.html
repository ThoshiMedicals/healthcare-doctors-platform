Introduction

A seasonal change in the nose or eyes raises a practical question: is this hay fever, and do I need an appointment? This guide explains those everyday words. It does not diagnose a cause and it does not describe treatment.

What Is It?

Hay fever is the everyday name for allergic rhinitis. Those words are not a diagnosis.

  • Sneezing you are calling hay fever
  • Itchy or watery eyes
  • A runny or blocked nose

This guide is not about a raised body temperature. A fever guide is a different topic.

Should I Be Concerned?

New sneezing, itchy eyes, or a runny nose is an everyday reason a general practice visit can help, including when hay fever is the word you are using. This guide does not judge how serious a symptom is.

When Should I Seek Medical Advice?

Seek medical advice when you would otherwise guess the cause, or when the symptoms are worsening. Do not use this page to decide that they can wait.

Urgent care. If symptoms are severe, rapidly worsening, or you believe there is a life-threatening emergency, call 000. Do not use the booking form for emergency care.

How Can a GP Help?

A general practitioner can listen to what has changed and decide whether an examination or a follow-up plan is appropriate. That assessment happens at a general practice visit. This guide does not name a practitioner and it does not describe a procedure.

Ask reception for a general practice appointment and say what has changed. This page does not choose an appointment type. If you are unsure which appointment to book, call reception on (07) 3217 3344.

Men and women can both book a general practice visit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is hayfever a different condition from hay fever?

No. Hayfever is only another spelling. Allergic rhinitis is the clinical name for those same everyday words.

Is hay fever the same as a cold?

No. A cold is a different question. This page cannot tell the two apart.
