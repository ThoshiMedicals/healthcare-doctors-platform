Introduction

Cholesterol is a fat-like substance used by the body. Blood tests usually report different types of cholesterol and triglycerides. A result makes more sense when considered alongside blood pressure, diabetes, smoking, family history and previous cardiovascular disease.

Why This Topic Matters

High LDL cholesterol can contribute to fatty deposits in blood vessels. Most people do not feel cholesterol rising. The reason for treatment is to reduce future cardiovascular risk, not simply to reach a number without context.

Signs, Symptoms or Situations Relevant to Patients

The following situations are worth recognising. They do not establish a diagnosis by themselves:

  • A blood test reports raised LDL cholesterol or triglycerides.
  • A close relative experienced heart disease at a young age or has a hereditary lipid disorder.
  • You have diabetes, kidney disease, high blood pressure or other cardiovascular risks.

When Should I Seek Medical Advice?

Book a GP review after an abnormal result, particularly when there is strong family history or multiple risks. Sudden chest pressure, arm or jaw pain, fainting or suspected stroke symptoms are emergencies and require 000, regardless of previous cholesterol results.

How Can a GP Help?

Your GP can interpret lipid results, estimate overall risk where applicable, discuss diet, exercise and smoking cessation, and explain when cholesterol-lowering medicines may help. Additional tests or specialist review may be appropriate for very high levels or suspected inherited conditions. Patients from Woolloongabba and nearby Brisbane inner-south communities can discuss the most appropriate next step with the practice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I feel high cholesterol?

Usually not. High cholesterol is generally detected through a blood test.

Does a high total cholesterol result always mean I need tablets?

Not necessarily. Decisions take your full risk profile and preferences into account.

How soon should cholesterol be rechecked?

The schedule depends on your results, treatment and cardiovascular risk; your GP can set the interval.
