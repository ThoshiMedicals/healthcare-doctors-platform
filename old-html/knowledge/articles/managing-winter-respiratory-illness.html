Introduction

Winter can bring more respiratory illness as people spend time indoors. Viruses such as influenza, RSV and other cold viruses may cause cough, sore throat, fever and fatigue. Most mild viral illnesses improve with time and supportive care, but some patients need closer monitoring.

Why This Topic Matters

Young babies, older adults, pregnant people and those with certain medical conditions can be at higher risk of complications. Antibiotics do not treat uncomplicated viral infections; assessment helps distinguish expected recovery from pneumonia, dehydration or another problem.

Signs, Symptoms or Situations Relevant to Patients

The following situations are worth recognising. They do not establish a diagnosis by themselves:

  • Cough, congestion, sore throat, body aches or a fever affecting usual activity.
  • Worsening breathlessness, chest discomfort, reduced drinking or signs of dehydration.
  • Symptoms that improve then worsen again, or fever that persists unexpectedly.

When Should I Seek Medical Advice?

Book a GP review for persistent fever, worsening cough or additional risk factors, and ask whether testing or antiviral medicines may be relevant. Seek urgent help for marked breathing difficulty, blue lips, confusion, severe dehydration or a seriously unwell baby; call 000 for life-threatening signs.

How Can a GP Help?

A GP can assess breathing and hydration, examine you when needed, and guide treatment or testing. They can advise about staying home while contagious, symptom relief, vaccination and when to return for review. Children and older adults may need different thresholds for reassessment. Patients from Woolloongabba and nearby Brisbane inner-south communities can discuss the most appropriate next step with the practice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will antibiotics help my winter cough?

Most winter respiratory infections are viral, so antibiotics do not help unless a bacterial infection is suspected or confirmed.

Should I attend in person with respiratory symptoms?

Contact the clinic first if you have infectious symptoms so the team can advise on safe attendance and appointment options.

Can influenza vaccination still help during winter?

Yes. It may still be worthwhile during the season depending on individual recommendations and vaccine availability.
