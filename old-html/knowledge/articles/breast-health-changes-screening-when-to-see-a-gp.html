Introduction

Breast health involves understanding what is normal for your body, noticing changes and using screening appropriately. A new breast symptom should be assessed even if a recent screening mammogram was normal; screening and investigating symptoms serve different purposes.

Why This Topic Matters

BreastScreen Australia invites eligible women aged 50–74 for a free mammogram every two years. Women aged 40–49 and 75 or older may also access free screening, but are generally not routinely invited. Family history and symptoms may change the recommended pathway.

Signs, Symptoms or Situations Relevant to Patients

The following situations are worth recognising. They do not establish a diagnosis by themselves:

  • A new lump or thickened area in a breast or armpit.
  • Nipple discharge, especially if bloody, or a newly inverted nipple.
  • Persistent skin dimpling, redness, swelling or a change in breast shape.

When Should I Seek Medical Advice?

Arrange a GP appointment promptly for a new lump, persistent breast change or unexplained nipple discharge. Do not wait for the next routine BreastScreen invitation. Sudden severe infection symptoms or rapidly worsening illness may require urgent care.

How Can a GP Help?

A GP can take a history, examine the area, review family risk and arrange appropriate imaging or referral when indicated. Your clinician can also explain routine screening eligibility. Screening mammograms are not a substitute for diagnostic investigation of a new symptom. Patients from Woolloongabba and nearby Brisbane inner-south communities can discuss the most appropriate next step with the practice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to wait until age 50 for a breast screen?

Women aged 40–49 may access free BreastScreen mammography, but screening decisions should take individual risks into account.

I had a normal mammogram. Should I ignore a new lump?

No. A new lump or change requires assessment even after normal screening.

Are all breast lumps cancer?

No. Many are benign, but examination and, where needed, imaging are important to identify the cause.
