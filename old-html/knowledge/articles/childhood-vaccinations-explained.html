Introduction

Vaccination protects children against serious infectious diseases and is scheduled at particular ages so protection is provided when it is most useful. Australia’s National Immunisation Program provides a current schedule from infancy through adulthood.

Why This Topic Matters

Routine immunisation reduces the chance of serious infections and helps protect the wider community. The Australian National Immunisation Program schedule is updated, and some children need additional vaccines because of risk factors.

Signs, Symptoms or Situations to Recognise

Parents may have questions about a missed dose, common short-term reactions or a child with allergies or an ongoing illness. An accurate record in the Australian Immunisation Register helps your GP identify the appropriate catch-up schedule.

  • Your child has missed or delayed routine vaccinations.
  • You are unsure about eligibility for funded or additional vaccines.
  • A possible severe reaction, such as breathing difficulty, requires immediate emergency assistance.

Use the current Australian schedule, not an old checklist

Vaccination recommendations change over time as evidence, available vaccines and public health programs change. The Australian Immunisation Handbook and National Immunisation Program Schedule are the appropriate sources for current guidance.

If a child has missed doses, started vaccination overseas or has an uncertain record, catch-up vaccination may be possible. The exact plan depends on age, previous doses and clinical circumstances.

  • Bring your child’s immunisation history to appointments where possible
  • Ask about catch-up vaccination if doses have been missed
  • Tell the clinician about significant allergies, previous vaccine reactions or immune conditions
  • Keep the Australian Immunisation Register record accurate

When Should I Seek Medical Advice?

Book a vaccination review if your child is due, has missed doses, has an overseas vaccination history or you are unsure what is needed. Seek urgent care after vaccination for severe breathing difficulty, collapse or other signs of a serious allergic reaction.

Urgent care: If symptoms are severe, rapidly worsening or you believe there is a life-threatening emergency, call 000.

How Can a GP Help?

A GP or vaccination-trained clinician can review the record, confirm what is due, discuss common side effects and precautions, administer appropriate vaccines and document them on the Australian Immunisation Register.

Families from Woolloongabba, Dutton Park, Stones Corner and surrounding areas can use general practice as a familiar first point of care for many childhood health concerns.

Frequently Asked Questions

What if my child has missed a vaccine?

A catch-up schedule can often be created. Bring any available records so previous doses can be considered.

Are all childhood vaccines given at the same visit?

The schedule groups some vaccines together, but the exact vaccines due depend on age, history and special-risk factors.

Where can I check the current schedule?

The Australian Government National Immunisation Program Schedule and Australian Immunisation Handbook provide current national guidance.
