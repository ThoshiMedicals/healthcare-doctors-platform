Introduction

A child’s first appointment at a new practice is easier when the GP has enough background to understand both the immediate concern and the child’s broader health. A small amount of preparation can make the visit more useful.

Why This Topic Matters

A prepared first visit helps the GP understand the child’s development, current concerns, previous care and immunisation history. It also creates an opportunity for parents to ask questions without feeling rushed.

Signs, Symptoms or Situations to Recognise

Useful information includes medicines, allergies, feeding or sleep concerns, recent symptoms and hospital letters. Bring your child’s health record and a list of questions; check whether a longer appointment would help.

  • Your child has persistent feeding, growth or developmental concerns.
  • You need a catch-up immunisation discussion.
  • You want a medical review of recurring symptoms rather than only reassurance.

Bring the information that helps build continuity

Useful information can include Medicare details, immunisation history, allergies, current medicines, important past illnesses or operations, specialist letters and any relevant growth or developmental information.

If you are attending for a specific symptom, note when it started, what makes it better or worse, fever readings, eating and drinking changes, and any treatments already tried. Photos or videos can sometimes help show intermittent symptoms such as a rash or unusual movement.

  • Bring immunisation and medication information
  • Write down the main concern and key questions
  • Allow extra time if there are several issues or a complex history
  • Bring comfort items for younger children if helpful

When Should I Seek Medical Advice?

If your child is acutely unwell, do not delay urgent assessment simply to wait for a routine first appointment. Severe breathing difficulty, unresponsiveness, significant injury or other emergencies require 000 or emergency care.

Urgent care: If symptoms are severe, rapidly worsening or you believe there is a life-threatening emergency, call 000.

How Can a GP Help?

The GP can establish the child’s history, address the current concern, review preventive needs and create a clearer record for future care. Ongoing visits can then build on the same history rather than starting again each time.

Families from Woolloongabba, Dutton Park, Stones Corner and surrounding areas can use general practice as a familiar first point of care for many childhood health concerns.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I book a longer appointment?

Consider a longer appointment if your child has several concerns, a complex history, developmental issues or extensive paperwork.

What if records are held at another practice?

Bring what you have and ask reception about the practice’s process for transferring relevant records.

Can I bring a list of questions?

Yes. A short prioritised list can help make sure the most important concerns are covered.
