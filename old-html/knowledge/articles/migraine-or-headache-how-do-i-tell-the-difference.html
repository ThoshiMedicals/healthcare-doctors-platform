Introduction

People often use the word “headache” for any head pain, but migraine has characteristic features and may need a different management approach. A diagnosis depends on the whole symptom history, not one symptom alone.

What Is It?

Migraine is a neurological condition that can cause moderate or severe headache, nausea and sensitivity to light or sound. Some people experience temporary visual or sensory symptoms called aura. Tension-type headache more often feels like pressure or tightness, although features can overlap.

  • Throbbing or disabling head pain
  • Nausea or sensitivity to light and sound
  • Visual disturbances before or during an episode

Should I Be Concerned?

A headache that repeatedly forces you to stop activities, involves nausea, or follows a similar disabling pattern may suggest migraine. New aura-like symptoms should not automatically be attributed to migraine, particularly if they differ from anything you have experienced before.

When Should I Seek Medical Advice?

Call 000 for new facial drooping, weakness, trouble speaking, sudden severe headache or other symptoms that could indicate stroke. Urgent assessment is also necessary for sudden vision loss, confusion or seizure, even if you have a history of migraine.

Urgent symptoms: If this is a life-threatening emergency, call 000. Do not use the booking form for emergency care.

How Can a GP Help?

A GP can discuss whether the pattern fits migraine, look for alternative causes and talk through acute treatment, prevention, medication overuse and practical triggers. If migraine is frequent or difficult to control, your GP may discuss preventive medicines or referral.

A headache diary, list of medicines and details of new associated symptoms can help make the consultation more useful. If unsure which appointment to choose, call reception on (07) 3217 3344.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can migraine happen without severe headache?

Yes. Some people have aura or other migraine-related symptoms with little or no head pain; new neurological symptoms still need medical assessment.

Are migraine and tension headache treated the same way?

Not always. The choice of medicine and prevention depends on the headache type, frequency, health history and other medicines.
