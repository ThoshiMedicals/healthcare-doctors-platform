Introduction

Neck stiffness or pain can make driving, sleeping and ordinary movement uncomfortable. Most episodes improve, but certain symptoms need medical attention.

This guide is general information for patients in Woolloongabba and neighbouring inner-south Brisbane communities. It does not replace an individual clinical assessment.

What Is It?

Neck pain refers to discomfort in the cervical spine and surrounding muscles, joints and soft tissues. It may follow an awkward sleeping position, prolonged static posture, muscle strain or an injury. Pain can also spread to the shoulders or upper back.

Some neck symptoms arise from irritation of nerves in the neck. A GP assessment considers the pattern of symptoms rather than assuming that every episode needs an X-ray or scan.

Should I Be Concerned?

Many short episodes of mild neck pain ease with time and gentle movement. Arrange a GP review if the pain is severe, increasing, recurrent, disturbing your sleep or preventing normal activities.

Neck pain after a fall or accident, or pain accompanied by fever or neurological symptoms, deserves more urgent attention. The important factors are the cause, associated symptoms and whether normal movement and strength are affected.

Common Symptoms and Situations

You might notice stiffness when turning your head, muscle tenderness, pain around the base of the skull, or aching that spreads into a shoulder. Some people also experience headaches.

Tingling, numbness, weakness or shooting pain down an arm can suggest nerve involvement and should be discussed with a clinician. Symptoms affecting balance, walking or hand coordination need prompt assessment.

If you have significant neck pain after trauma, do not try to force the neck to move or test it with stretches.

When Should I Seek Medical Advice?

Call Triple Zero (000) for severe neck pain after major trauma, or neck pain with chest pain, serious breathing difficulty, collapse or other immediately life-threatening symptoms. Keep movement limited after a significant injury until emergency help arrives.

Seek urgent same-day medical assessment for neck pain with fever and marked stiffness, new arm or leg weakness, walking or balance problems, new bowel or bladder changes, or other worrying neurological symptoms.

Make a routine GP appointment if neck pain continues, recurs, is getting worse or limits work, sleep and daily activities.

Urgent symptoms: If someone is in immediate danger or has life-threatening symptoms, call 000. Do not use online booking for emergency care.

How Can a GP Help?

Your GP can ask when pain started, whether there was an injury, what movements affect it, and whether there are symptoms in your arms or legs. Examination may include neck movement, strength, sensation and reflexes when indicated.

Care depends on the cause and may involve advice about comfortable movement, pacing, suitable pain relief, workplace habits and physiotherapy. Imaging or specialist referral is considered when the history or examination suggests it would change management.

What Happens at the Appointment?

Tell the GP whether symptoms followed an accident, where the pain travels, any tingling or weakness, and what you have already tried. Note whether symptoms are worse at work, overnight or with certain movements.

Your GP may discuss an activity plan and when to seek review. Ask about the safest way to return to exercise, driving, lifting and work. A scan is not automatic for uncomplicated neck pain.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a stiff neck always serious?

No. Mild stiffness is common. However, a stiff neck with fever, severe headache, confusion or neurological symptoms needs urgent medical assessment.

Should I get a scan for neck pain?

Not everyone needs imaging. Your GP will consider trauma, weakness, numbness, examination findings and whether imaging would help guide care.

Can neck pain cause headaches?

Yes. Some neck conditions can be associated with headaches, but headaches have many causes. New severe or unusual headaches require assessment.

Can I continue moving when my neck hurts?

Gentle, comfortable movement often helps uncomplicated neck pain, but the advice differs after injury or when red-flag symptoms are present. Seek individual advice if unsure.
