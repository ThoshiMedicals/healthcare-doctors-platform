Introduction

Preventive healthcare creates opportunities to address health risks before they become urgent. For men who tend to seek care only when symptoms are disruptive, a planned GP review can help bring screening and risk factors back into view.

Why This Topic Matters

Conditions such as hypertension and high cholesterol can develop without obvious symptoms. Planned preventive care uses evidence and personal risk to identify meaningful actions rather than offering blanket annual testing.

Signs, Symptoms or Situations to Recognise

Reasons to schedule a review include smoking, diabetes in the family, previous abnormal results, long-term medicines or missed screening invitations. A consultation can also focus on sleep, mental health and physical activity.

  • Your risk factors have changed since the last review.
  • You are unsure whether screening or immunisations are due.
  • You want to set achievable health goals and arrange follow-up.

Prevention is about informed action, not unnecessary testing

Preventive care may include blood pressure, cardiovascular and diabetes risk, vaccination, bowel screening, skin health, mental wellbeing and lifestyle. Which of these matters now depends on age, risk and previous results.

A continuing relationship with a GP also helps track changes over time. This can make it easier to notice trends in blood pressure, weight, medicines, mental health or chronic conditions rather than treating each appointment as an isolated event.

  • Identify modifiable risk factors early
  • Keep national screening participation up to date
  • Use vaccination and lifestyle advice to reduce preventable illness
  • Create a follow-up plan rather than relying on memory

When Should I Seek Medical Advice?

Consider a preventive appointment if it has been a long time since your last review, your family history has changed, you have new risk factors or you are unsure which screening programs apply to you.

Urgent care: If symptoms are severe, rapidly worsening or you believe there is a life-threatening emergency, call 000.

How Can a GP Help?

A GP can prioritise what is relevant, explain screening benefits and limitations, support risk reduction and arrange follow-up for abnormal results or chronic disease risks.

Patients in Woolloongabba, Kangaroo Point, Greenslopes and nearby communities can discuss men's health concerns with a GP in a confidential setting.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is preventive care only for older men?

No. Prevention is relevant throughout adulthood, although the priorities change with age and risk.

What if I feel healthy?

Many preventive checks are designed for people without symptoms, because some risks such as high blood pressure may not be noticeable.

Can I discuss several preventive issues in one visit?

Often yes, but a longer appointment may be useful if there are multiple concerns or a complex history.
