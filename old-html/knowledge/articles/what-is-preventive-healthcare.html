Introduction

Preventive healthcare aims to reduce avoidable illness, identify important risks early and help people stay well for longer. It includes screening and vaccination, but also involves reviewing lifestyle, family history and risks that may change over time.

Why This Topic Matters

Preventive healthcare aims to reduce illness and identify risks early. It is tailored to a person’s age, family history and circumstances; doing more tests does not always mean better care.

Signs, Symptoms or Situations to Recognise

Opportunities include checking blood pressure, discussing cardiovascular risk, reviewing vaccination status, and considering cancer screening invitations. New symptoms need diagnostic review, not only a preventive check.

  • You want help deciding which tests are actually useful.
  • Your family history or health risks have changed.
  • You have missed screening or immunisation milestones.

Prevention is planned care before problems become urgent

Examples include blood pressure checks, recommended cancer screening, vaccination, cardiovascular and diabetes risk assessment, smoking cessation and support with nutrition, physical activity, sleep and mental wellbeing.

Good preventive care is selective. Tests and screening should have a clear evidence-based purpose, because more testing is not automatically better and can sometimes lead to unnecessary follow-up.

  • Use national screening programs where eligible
  • Review vaccination according to age and risk
  • Discuss family history and changing risk factors
  • Build prevention into an ongoing GP relationship

When Should I Seek Medical Advice?

Consider a preventive appointment if you are unsure what screening is due, have not reviewed your health risks for some time or your family, medical or lifestyle circumstances have changed.

Urgent care: If symptoms are severe, rapidly worsening or you believe there is a life-threatening emergency, call 000.

How Can a GP Help?

A GP can review your personal risks, identify appropriate screening and vaccination, discuss realistic risk reduction and create a plan for follow-up rather than treating prevention as a one-off event.

Preventive care can be built into routine GP visits for patients across Woolloongabba and Brisbane's inner south rather than treated as a one-off checklist.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is preventive healthcare only screening?

No. It also includes vaccination, risk-factor management, lifestyle support and monitoring of emerging health risks.

Do I need every test every year?

No. Testing should be based on age, risk, symptoms, previous results and current clinical guidance.

Can prevention be part of normal GP care?

Yes. Routine appointments can identify preventive needs, with a separate longer review arranged when more time is useful.
