Introduction

The purpose of long-term diabetes care is to reduce avoidable complications while supporting quality of life. Small, realistic changes and regular review can make a meaningful difference.

What Is It?

Diabetes can affect the eyes, kidneys, nerves, feet and cardiovascular system. Risk is influenced by glucose exposure over time, blood pressure, cholesterol, smoking and other factors. Not everyone develops complications, and many risks can be reduced.

  • Unhealed foot wounds
  • Changes in eyesight or sensation
  • High blood pressure or cholesterol alongside diabetes

Should I Be Concerned?

Book review for persistent high readings, changes in sight, numbness, foot skin breaks, new swelling or difficulty managing medicines. Prompt care for a foot wound can be particularly important in diabetes.

When Should I Seek Medical Advice?

Seek same-day urgent care for a foot infection that spreads or causes fever, sudden vision change or severe illness. Call 000 for chest pain suggesting a heart attack, stroke symptoms, unconsciousness or severe metabolic symptoms.

Urgent symptoms: If this is a life-threatening emergency, call 000. Do not use the booking form for emergency care.

How Can a GP Help?

A GP can review overall cardiovascular risk, tailor glucose and blood-pressure treatment, support smoking cessation where relevant, and schedule eye, kidney and foot screening. Referrals and follow-up should be based on identified risk rather than a fixed checklist for everyone.

Bring recent glucose results if available, medicine details and questions about long-term monitoring or referrals. If unsure which appointment to choose, call reception on (07) 3217 3344.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are complications inevitable?

No. Many people live well with diabetes, especially when risk factors are monitored and managed consistently.

Can regular foot care help?

Yes. Checking skin, footwear and sensation and treating injuries early can help reduce complications. Ask for advice tailored to your risk.
