Introduction

Regular GP care is useful because health changes over time. A check-up can create space to review risks that may not cause symptoms, update prevention and make sure ongoing conditions and medicines still fit your needs.

Why This Topic Matters

Regular follow-up creates continuity and helps address evolving risk, especially when managing long-term conditions or medicines. The right interval varies; a healthy person may not need the same checks as someone with diabetes or cardiovascular disease.

Signs, Symptoms or Situations to Recognise

Review becomes important after new symptoms, changing blood pressure, a new diagnosis, repeated medicine changes or gaps in recommended screening. Bring recent results where possible.

  • You have chronic conditions that need monitoring.
  • You are unsure how often an existing medicine needs review.
  • You want to revisit health goals after lifestyle or family-history changes.

Continuity makes small changes easier to notice

Blood pressure, vaccination status, screening eligibility, weight, medicines, cardiovascular risk and chronic disease markers can change gradually. When a GP knows your history, new results can be interpreted against the broader pattern.

The right frequency is not the same for everyone. A healthy young adult with few risk factors may need less frequent preventive review than someone with multiple chronic conditions, several medicines or a significant family history.

  • Use check-ups to update prevention, not to order every possible test
  • Review medicines and new specialist recommendations
  • Track risk factors over time
  • Agree on when the next review is actually needed

When Should I Seek Medical Advice?

Book a review if you have not had preventive care for some time, have new family history, new medicines or a chronic condition that needs monitoring. New symptoms should not be postponed until a routine check-up.

Urgent care: If symptoms are severe, rapidly worsening or you believe there is a life-threatening emergency, call 000.

How Can a GP Help?

A GP can connect current symptoms, past history and prevention, decide which checks are appropriate and maintain a follow-up plan over time.

Preventive care can be built into routine GP visits for patients across Woolloongabba and Brisbane's inner south rather than treated as a one-off checklist.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I have a check-up?

There is no single interval for everyone. Your age, risks, conditions and previous results determine what is sensible.

Should I wait for symptoms before seeing a GP?

Not always. Preventive care is specifically designed to address some risks before symptoms develop.

Can a check-up review my medicines?

Yes. Medication review is an important part of ongoing care, especially when several medicines or prescribers are involved.
