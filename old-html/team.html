How the practice works together

One practice, different roles, shared responsibility.

Not every patient will need every part of the team. The role of the practice is to make the pathway clear, so each person is supported by the right team member at the right time.

1. Access and appointmentsReception helps you book, arrive, understand practical requirements and find the right administrative pathway.

2. Clinical assessmentYour GP usually leads diagnosis, treatment decisions, preventive care and coordination of medical care.

3. Clinical supportNursing support may assist with treatment-room care, preventive-health activities and follow-up alongside the GP team.

4. Extended careAllied health may support particular rehabilitation, movement or wellbeing needs when appropriate to your care.

5. Quality and governancePractice management maintains the systems for privacy, safety, quality, feedback and reliable day-to-day operations.

Team-based care

Why working together matters.

Continuity gives care context. Returning to a practice that knows your history can reduce repetition and help healthcare decisions build on what is already known.

Different roles support different needs. Doctors, nursing support, allied health, reception and practice management each contribute to the patient experience in different ways.

Coordination matters when care becomes more complex. Clear pathways between appointments, follow-up, referrals and related services help patients understand what happens next.

Support includes the practical parts of care. Booking, access, privacy, results, after-hours information and feedback processes all shape whether healthcare feels dependable.

Continuity of care

Why being known over time matters.

Continuity means care can build on what is already known about you rather than starting again at every visit. Where possible, seeing a familiar GP can help place new symptoms, medicines, test results, referrals and life changes into the context of your longer health story.

Less repetitionA familiar clinical history can reduce the need to retell the same background at every appointment.

Better contextChanges in your health can be considered against previous consultations, investigations and treatment decisions.

More coordinated follow-upResults, referrals and longer-term plans are easier to connect when care is followed over time.

A team that still works togetherIf your usual practitioner is unavailable, shared practice systems help the wider healthcare team support the next step while maintaining appropriate privacy and clinical responsibility.
