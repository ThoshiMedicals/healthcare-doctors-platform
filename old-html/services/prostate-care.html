Procedure information

What this care involves.

Prostate symptoms can have several causes, including benign enlargement, infection or other conditions. PSA testing can provide useful information in some situations but does not diagnose prostate cancer by itself. Decisions about testing are best made after discussing symptoms, age, family history and personal preferences.

What happens during the appointment?

Your doctor will take a history, review urinary or other symptoms and may perform a physical examination. Depending on your situation, care may include urine tests, a PSA blood test, imaging or referral to a urologist.

This page provides general information only. The treating clinician will explain what is appropriate for your individual circumstances and obtain consent where a procedure is planned.

Before your appointment

How to book and prepare.

How to book

Book a GP appointment. If your concern involves several symptoms, previous PSA results or a detailed discussion about testing, ask reception whether a longer appointment may be useful.

Before your appointment

Bring previous PSA results, urology reports, a current medicines list and information about family history if available. If a PSA test is planned, your clinician or pathology provider may give preparation instructions because some activities or conditions can temporarily affect PSA levels.

After your appointment

Follow-up and aftercare.

Your doctor will review results in context. A raised PSA does not by itself mean cancer, and further assessment may include repeat testing, imaging or specialist referral. Follow the review plan provided by your clinician.

Patient questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a PSA test diagnose prostate cancer?

No. PSA is one part of assessment. A result must be interpreted with symptoms, risk factors and other clinical information.

Should every man have a PSA test?

The decision is individual. Your doctor can discuss potential benefits and limitations based on your age, symptoms, family history and preferences.

What symptoms should I discuss with a doctor?

Changes in urinary flow, frequency, urgency, difficulty passing urine, blood in urine or semen, or other new symptoms should be assessed.

What happens if my PSA is elevated?

Your doctor will consider possible causes and may recommend repeat testing, imaging or specialist assessment depending on the result and your risk profile.
