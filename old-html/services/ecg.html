ECG (Electrocardiogram)

An electrocardiogram (ECG) is a simple, painless test that records the electrical activity of the heart.

Procedure information

What this care involves.

An ECG can provide useful information about heart rhythm and electrical activity. It may be used when assessing symptoms such as palpitations or chest discomfort, monitoring some medicines, or investigating other heart-related concerns. An ECG is only one part of clinical assessment.

What happens during the appointment?

Small adhesive electrodes are placed on the chest and limbs and connected to the ECG machine. You lie still while the tracing is recorded. The test itself is brief and does not send electricity into your body.

This page provides general information only. The treating clinician will explain what is appropriate for your individual circumstances and obtain consent where a procedure is planned.

Before your appointment

How to book and prepare.

How to book

Book a GP appointment or call reception if an ECG has already been requested. The team can confirm whether the ECG is performed as part of the consultation or requires a specific appointment sequence.

Before your appointment

No special preparation is usually required for a resting ECG. Wear clothing that allows access to the chest and limbs. Skin may need to be cleaned or small areas shaved so electrodes can attach properly.

After your appointment

Follow-up and aftercare.

There is usually no recovery time after a resting ECG. A clinician will interpret the tracing together with your symptoms and history. Depending on the findings, you may need no further action, repeat assessment, blood tests, monitoring, imaging or specialist review.

Patient questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Does an ECG hurt?

A resting ECG is painless. Adhesive electrodes are placed on the skin to record the heart’s electrical activity.

How long does an ECG take?

The recording itself is brief, but allow time for preparation, the clinical assessment and discussion of results.

Can a normal ECG rule out every heart problem?

No. An ECG records the heart during the test and must be interpreted with symptoms and other information. Further testing may still be needed.

What if I have severe chest pain now?

Do not wait for a routine ECG appointment. Call Triple Zero (000) for severe or life-threatening symptoms.
