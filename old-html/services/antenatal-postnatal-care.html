Procedure information

What this care involves.

Regular pregnancy care helps monitor the health of the pregnant person and baby, coordinate recommended tests and referrals, and provide opportunities to discuss medicines, vaccination, nutrition, mental health and birth planning. After birth, follow-up helps address recovery, feeding, contraception, mental health and ongoing medical needs.

What happens during the appointment?

GP care may include confirming pregnancy, reviewing medical history and medicines, arranging or discussing tests and scans, monitoring general health, coordinating shared maternity care where appropriate, and providing postnatal reviews. The exact maternity pathway depends on your preferences, health needs and hospital or specialist arrangements.

This page provides general information only. The treating clinician will explain what is appropriate for your individual circumstances and obtain consent where a procedure is planned.

Before your appointment

How to book and prepare.

How to book

Book a GP appointment as early as practical after pregnancy is confirmed. If you are seeking shared antenatal care, ask reception to help identify a practitioner with relevant experience. For postnatal care, book when advised by your maternity team or sooner if you have concerns.

Before your appointment

Bring pregnancy records, scan or test results, your medicines and supplements list, and details of your planned maternity hospital or obstetric care if known. After birth, bring relevant discharge information and any concerns about your physical recovery, mood, feeding or medicines.

After your appointment

Follow-up and aftercare.

Your GP may arrange further antenatal appointments, pathology, imaging or referral as part of your maternity pathway. Postnatal follow-up may include physical recovery, mental health, contraception, breastfeeding or feeding concerns, and management of ongoing conditions.

Costs

Know about fees before you attend.

Exact additional fees are confirmed before treatment.

Medicare-eligible GP consultations are bulk billed in line with the practice billing policy. Pregnancy-related ultrasounds, pathology, genetic screening, hospital, obstetric or allied health services may be provided and billed separately by external providers. Reception can confirm any practice charge before booking; external providers set their own fees.

Patient questions

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I first see a GP in pregnancy?

Pregnancy, Birth and Baby recommends arranging antenatal care once pregnancy is confirmed. Early review helps establish your care pathway and organise appropriate tests.

Can the practice provide all maternity care?

The model of care varies. GP care may form part of shared care, while hospital, midwifery or obstetric services provide other parts of pregnancy and birth care.

What can I discuss at a postnatal appointment?

Physical recovery, mood, feeding concerns, contraception, medicines and any new symptoms can all be discussed.

What if I have an urgent pregnancy concern?

Contact your maternity service or seek urgent medical care according to the advice provided for your pregnancy. Call 000 for a life-threatening emergency.
