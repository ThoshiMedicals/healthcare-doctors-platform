Medical Procedures

Ear Care & Ear Syringing

Ear care may include examination of the ear canal, treatment advice for wax build-up, and ear irrigation (often called ear syringing) when it is clinically appropriate.

Procedure information

What this care involves.

Blocked ears, reduced hearing, discomfort or a feeling of fullness can sometimes be caused by ear wax, but other ear conditions can cause similar symptoms. The ear should be assessed before irrigation because syringing is not appropriate when there is certain ear disease, injury or infection.

What happens during the appointment?

A clinician examines the ear first. If wax is the likely cause and irrigation is suitable, body-temperature water may be used to gently flush wax from the ear canal. If the wax cannot be safely removed, another approach or referral may be recommended.

This page provides general information only. The treating clinician will explain what is appropriate for your individual circumstances and obtain consent where a procedure is planned.

Before your appointment

Wax-softening drops may be recommended before irrigation, but they are not appropriate for everyone. Follow the instructions given by the practice rather than starting drops if you have a history of eardrum perforation, ear surgery, infection or other significant ear problems.

After your appointment

Follow-up and aftercare.

Mild temporary discomfort can occur. Contact the practice if you develop persistent pain, discharge, dizziness, worsening hearing or other concerning symptoms. If wax cannot be removed safely, a referral may be recommended.

Patient questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Do all blocked ears need syringing?

No. Ear blockage has different causes, and irrigation is not suitable for every ear. The ear should be assessed first.

Should I use wax-softening drops before my appointment?

Only if the practice or your clinician recommends them for you. Tell the team about previous eardrum perforation, ear surgery or infection.

Can cotton buds remove ear wax?

Objects placed into the ear canal can push wax deeper or injure the ear. Healthdirect advises against trying to pick wax out with cotton buds or other objects.

What if the wax cannot be removed?

The clinician may recommend another treatment method or referral to an ear, nose and throat service.
