What is this service?

A remote consultation for suitable healthcare needs.

Telehealth allows selected consultations to take place remotely rather than in the medical centre. Depending on the appointment type and current practice arrangements, this may be by telephone or another approved telehealth method.

It can be useful for some follow-up consultations, results discussions, medication reviews and care planning. It is not suitable for every concern.

If a practitioner needs to examine you, check observations, perform a procedure or arrange immediate testing, you may be asked to attend in person.

Why does it matter?

Convenience can help patients stay connected to care.

For suitable concerns, telehealth can reduce travel and make follow-up easier while maintaining contact with the practice that knows your history.

Clinical suitability remains more important than convenience. A remote consultation may need to be followed by an in-person appointment.

How can we help?

Remote care when clinically suitable.

When clinically appropriate, telehealth may support:

  • Selected follow-up consultations
  • Discussion of results and next steps
  • Medication review and prescription decisions where clinically appropriate
  • Review of an existing care plan
  • Mental health follow-up where suitable
  • Discussion of referrals and ongoing care
  • Transition to an in-person appointment when examination or testing is required

Understanding your care

Prepare as you would for an in-person appointment.

Choose a private place where you can speak openly, keep your medication list and relevant information nearby, and make sure the practice has your correct contact details.

Telehealth is not an emergency service. If symptoms are severe, rapidly worsening or potentially life-threatening, seek urgent assessment rather than waiting for a routine telehealth appointment.

This information is general in nature. Your practitioner can provide advice based on your individual health history and circumstances.

What should I do next?

Choose telehealth only when it is suitable for the reason you need care.

If a telehealth appointment is offered for your reason for care, book the appropriate appointment type. If you are unsure whether telehealth is suitable, contact reception for booking guidance.

  • Use a private, reliable phone or device and keep it available around the appointment time.
  • Have your medication list, recent results and questions ready.
  • Be prepared to attend in person if the practitioner decides an examination or test is needed.
  • For a life-threatening emergency, call Triple Zero (000).

Planning your appointment

Booking, preparation, follow-up and costs.

How to book

Book through the available HotDoc options or call reception. Telehealth is used only when the clinician considers remote care appropriate for the problem being managed.

Before your appointment

Be in a private place, have your medicines and relevant information available, and keep your phone nearby. Some concerns will require a face-to-face examination instead.

After your appointment & follow-up

The clinician will explain any tests, prescriptions, referrals or in-person follow-up that are needed. The practice may use HotDoc for communication after the consultation.

Costs

Standard Medicare-eligible GP consultations are 100% bulk billed at Woolloongabba Medical Centre for patients with a valid Medicare card. Procedures, consumables, medicines or devices, reports and other non-Medicare services may attract additional fees. Reception will confirm the current practice charge before treatment or booking wherever practical. External providers set and bill their own fees.

Patient questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What can telehealth be used for?

Telehealth may be suitable for selected follow-up consultations, medication reviews, results discussions and healthcare planning, depending on clinical circumstances.

Will every concern be suitable for telehealth?

No. Some concerns require a physical examination, procedure, observations or other in-person assessment. Your doctor will advise if a face-to-face visit is needed.

Can prescriptions be provided after telehealth?

Prescribing depends on the medication, your clinical history and the doctor’s assessment. Some prescriptions require further review or an in-person consultation.

What if I need urgent medical help?

Telehealth is not an emergency service. For severe or rapidly worsening symptoms, seek urgent assessment. For a life-threatening emergency, call Triple Zero (000).
