Procedure information

What this care involves.

An iron infusion delivers iron directly into a vein. It may be considered when oral iron is not suitable, has not worked well enough, is not absorbed adequately, or when iron needs to be replaced more quickly. A clinician must first review the reason for low iron and confirm that an infusion is appropriate.

What happens during the appointment?

A nurse or doctor inserts an intravenous line, and the prescribed iron is given through a drip. You will be observed during the infusion and for the period the clinical team considers appropriate afterwards. Tell the team immediately if you feel unwell, develop pain around the IV site, or notice symptoms of a reaction.

This page provides general information only. The treating clinician will explain what is appropriate for your individual circumstances and obtain consent where a procedure is planned.

Before your appointment

How to book and prepare.

How to book

Please contact reception before booking an iron infusion. A GP review and recent blood results are usually needed first so the clinician can confirm the indication, the medicine and dose, and whether the infusion can be safely provided at the practice.

Before your appointment

Bring relevant recent pathology results if they are not already available to the practice, a current medicines list, and information about previous reactions or allergies. Depending on the treatment plan, you may be asked to obtain the prescribed iron medicine from a pharmacy before the infusion; the practice will confirm this with you.

After your appointment

Follow-up and aftercare.

Most people can resume normal activities after a routine infusion unless the treating clinician advises otherwise. Follow-up blood tests and/or a review appointment may be arranged to confirm that iron stores and haemoglobin have responded and to address the underlying cause of iron deficiency.

Costs

Know about fees before you attend.

Exact additional fees are confirmed before treatment.

The standard Medicare-eligible GP consultation is bulk billed in line with the practice billing policy. An iron infusion may involve an additional procedure/infusion fee, consumables, prescribed iron medicine and/or a longer appointment. Reception will confirm the current practice charge before the infusion is booked wherever practical. Pharmacy and external pathology charges are separate and are set by those providers.

Patient questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I book an iron infusion without seeing a doctor first?

Usually an assessment and recent blood results are needed before an infusion can be arranged. Contact reception so the team can confirm the correct sequence of appointments.

Do I need to fast?

Routine fasting is not usually required for an iron infusion, but follow the instructions given by your clinician or the practice because your individual circumstances may differ.

Will I need a blood test afterwards?

Follow-up blood testing is commonly used to assess the response to treatment. Your clinician will tell you when this should occur based on your treatment plan.

What if I feel unwell during the infusion?

Tell the nurse or doctor immediately. The clinical team will assess you and respond appropriately.
