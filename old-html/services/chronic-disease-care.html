What is this service?

Ongoing care for long-term health conditions.

Chronic disease care supports people living with health conditions that need ongoing monitoring, treatment or coordination over time. Examples can include diabetes, heart and vascular conditions, asthma, COPD, arthritis and other complex or long-term health needs.

General practice often acts as the central point for bringing together medicines, investigations, specialist advice, allied health input and the patient’s own goals.

Why does it matter?

Long-term conditions benefit from care that evolves over time.

Symptoms, medicines, test results and personal circumstances can change. Regular review helps keep the care plan current, identify complications earlier and coordinate the different professionals involved in your care.

Continuity is particularly valuable when you have more than one condition or take several medicines.

How can we help?

Monitoring, planning and coordination.

Depending on your condition and individual needs, care may include:

  • Regular clinical review and monitoring
  • Medication review and treatment planning
  • Chronic disease management planning where clinically appropriate
  • Review of pathology, imaging and specialist correspondence
  • Coordination with specialists and allied health practitioners
  • Support for self-management, lifestyle goals and risk reduction
  • Follow-up after hospital or specialist care
  • Preventive healthcare relevant to your long-term condition

Understanding your care

Good chronic care is coordinated care.

Keeping an accurate medication list and bringing recent specialist letters, discharge summaries or home monitoring information can help your GP see the whole picture.

If several conditions or care plans need review, ask reception whether a longer appointment would be more suitable.

This information is general in nature. Your practitioner can provide advice based on your individual health history and circumstances.

What should I do next?

Plan a review before problems become urgent.

Book a review with your regular GP or a practitioner whose verified interests match your condition. Regular follow-up is usually more useful than waiting until a problem becomes urgent.

  • Bring an up-to-date medication list, including non-prescription medicines if relevant.
  • Bring recent specialist letters, results or home monitoring records you have been asked to keep.
  • Ask whether a longer appointment is appropriate for complex or multiple conditions.
  • Seek urgent medical care for sudden or severe symptoms; call 000 for a life-threatening emergency.

Planning your appointment

Booking, preparation, follow-up and costs.

How to book

Book a GP appointment and ask for a longer appointment if several conditions, medicines or care-plan issues need review.

Before your appointment

Bring an up-to-date medicines list, home monitoring records if relevant, recent specialist letters and any questions about your care plan.

After your appointment & follow-up

Chronic care often involves planned review rather than one-off treatment. Follow-up may include repeat testing, medication review, allied health referral or a structured care plan.

Costs

Standard Medicare-eligible GP consultations are 100% bulk billed at Woolloongabba Medical Centre for patients with a valid Medicare card. Procedures, consumables, medicines or devices, reports and other non-Medicare services may attract additional fees. Reception will confirm the current practice charge before treatment or booking wherever practical. External providers set and bill their own fees.

Patient questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need regular reviews for chronic illness?

Regular monitoring allows healthcare plans to be reviewed as symptoms, treatment, test results and personal circumstances change.

Can several chronic conditions be managed together?

Yes. General practice often coordinates care across multiple conditions, medications and healthcare providers.

What should I bring to a chronic disease review?

Bring an up-to-date medication list, recent specialist letters or results you have received, and any home monitoring information your doctor has asked you to record.

Can a GP coordinate referrals or allied health care?

Where clinically appropriate, your GP can discuss referrals and coordinated care options. Eligibility for Medicare-funded programs depends on current requirements.
