What is this service?

A place to discuss the health issues that matter to you.

Men’s health in general practice covers both preventive care and assessment of new or ongoing concerns. This can include cardiovascular and metabolic risk, mental wellbeing, sexual and reproductive health, urinary or prostate-related symptoms and healthy ageing.

You do not need to wait for a problem to become severe before discussing it with a GP. Routine health reviews can help identify risks and create a plan for follow-up.

Why does it matter?

Preventive care supports long-term health planning.

Some health risks develop gradually and may not cause obvious symptoms early. A GP can consider your age, family history, medicines, lifestyle and previous results when discussing what checks or follow-up may be appropriate.

Continuity also makes it easier to revisit sensitive concerns over time with a practitioner who understands your history.

How can we help?

Support for prevention, symptoms and long-term health.

Depending on your health needs, care may include:

  • General health assessment and preventive care
  • Cardiovascular and metabolic risk review
  • Men’s sexual and reproductive health discussions
  • Urinary or prostate-related concerns
  • Mental health and wellbeing
  • Management of diabetes and other long-term conditions
  • Lifestyle and healthy-ageing discussions
  • Referral to specialist care when clinically appropriate

Understanding your care

Preventive healthcare is personal.

There is no single set of tests that is right for every man. Screening and health checks should be based on your age, symptoms, family history and individual risk factors.

If there is a symptom you have been putting off discussing, it is appropriate to raise it with your GP. You can ask reception about practitioner availability without describing private clinical details.

This information is general in nature. Your practitioner can provide advice based on your individual health history and circumstances.

What should I do next?

Book a health review or discuss the concern you have been putting off.

Book a GP appointment for a new concern, preventive health review or ongoing condition. If you have several issues to discuss, consider a longer appointment.

  • Choose a practitioner with a verified men’s health interest if that is important to you.
  • Bring your medication list and relevant recent results or specialist letters.
  • Tell your GP about important family history or changes in symptoms.
  • For severe or life-threatening symptoms, seek urgent care and call 000 when appropriate.

Planning your appointment

Booking, preparation, follow-up and costs.

How to book

Book a GP appointment. Ask for a longer appointment if you have several concerns or want a detailed preventive health or prostate discussion.

Before your appointment

Bring a current medicines list and relevant previous test results, including PSA results or specialist letters if available.

After your appointment & follow-up

Follow-up may include test results, lifestyle or medication review, repeat assessment, mental health support or referral to another health professional.

Costs

Standard Medicare-eligible GP consultations are 100% bulk billed at Woolloongabba Medical Centre for patients with a valid Medicare card. Procedures, consumables, medicines or devices, reports and other non-Medicare services may attract additional fees. Reception will confirm the current practice charge before treatment or booking wherever practical. External providers set and bill their own fees.

Patient questions

Frequently Asked Questions

When should men have routine health checks?

The timing and content of health checks varies with age, family history, lifestyle and existing conditions. A GP can help plan appropriate preventive care for your circumstances.

Can I discuss prostate or sexual health concerns with a GP?

Yes. These concerns can be discussed confidentially. Your doctor can assess symptoms and advise whether tests, treatment or referral are appropriate.

Can men discuss mental health during a general appointment?

Yes. Mental wellbeing is an important part of primary healthcare and can be discussed with your GP.

What if I have several concerns to cover?

Ask reception whether a longer appointment would be more appropriate so there is enough time to discuss the issues safely and thoroughly.
