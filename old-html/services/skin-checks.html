What is this service?

A skin check is a clinical assessment of your skin and areas of concern.

Skin checks can focus on a particular spot or involve a broader review depending on your reason for attending, history and risk factors. A GP can assess lesions and skin changes, discuss what they may represent and recommend an appropriate next step.

If a lesion needs further assessment, the next step may include monitoring, a procedure such as biopsy or excision where appropriate, or referral for specialist care.

Why does it matter?

Changes in the skin are worth assessing early.

Many skin spots and lesions are not cancer, but new, changing or unusual areas should not be ignored. Clinical assessment can help distinguish changes that can be monitored from those that need further investigation or treatment.

Your individual risk can be influenced by factors such as previous skin cancer, sun exposure, skin type, family history and the appearance or behaviour of a lesion.

How can we help?

Assessment, monitoring and next-step planning.

Depending on your needs and clinical assessment, care may include:

  • Assessment of new or changing spots and lesions
  • Review of areas that itch, bleed, crust, change shape or otherwise concern you
  • Discussion of personal and family skin-cancer history
  • Clinical examination and risk-based follow-up planning
  • Minor procedures such as biopsy or excision when clinically appropriate and available
  • Referral for specialist assessment when required
  • Preventive advice, including sun-protection discussions
  • Ongoing review where your practitioner recommends follow-up

Understanding your care

Know your skin and seek assessment when something changes.

Between appointments, being familiar with your own skin can help you notice a spot or lesion that is new or changing. A photograph may help document a change over time, but it does not replace clinical assessment.

There is no single skin-check interval that is right for everyone. Your practitioner can recommend follow-up based on your individual history, examination and risk factors.

This information is general in nature. Your practitioner can provide advice based on your individual health history and circumstances.

What should I do next?

Book a skin-check appointment for new, changing or concerning areas.

  • Tell reception if you want a focused lesion review or a broader skin-check appointment so appropriate time can be considered.
  • Bring information about previous skin cancers, biopsies or relevant specialist reports if available.
  • Avoid delaying assessment of a lesion that is changing rapidly, bleeding repeatedly or otherwise causing concern.
  • Call 000 for a life-threatening emergency; skin concerns that are urgent but not life-threatening should be assessed promptly through an appropriate clinical service.

Planning your appointment

Booking, preparation, follow-up and costs.

How to book

Book a skin-check appointment or ask reception which appointment type is appropriate. Mention a rapidly changing or concerning lesion when booking.

Before your appointment

If possible, avoid heavy makeup, nail polish over a concerning nail lesion, or clothing that makes the area difficult to examine. Bring previous skin-cancer history if not already known to the practice.

After your appointment & follow-up

Your clinician will explain whether routine observation, photography, biopsy, excision or referral is recommended and when the next review should occur.

Costs

Standard Medicare-eligible GP consultations are 100% bulk billed at Woolloongabba Medical Centre for patients with a valid Medicare card. Procedures, consumables, medicines or devices, reports and other non-Medicare services may attract additional fees. Reception will confirm the current practice charge before treatment or booking wherever practical. External providers set and bill their own fees.

Patient questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What happens during a skin check?

Your practitioner will ask about the reason for the visit and relevant history, examine the area or areas of concern and discuss whether monitoring, a procedure, referral or another next step is appropriate.

Do I need a referral for a GP skin check?

No referral is generally required to book a GP appointment at the practice. If specialist assessment is needed after your review, your GP can discuss referral options.

Can a GP biopsy or remove a skin lesion?

Some practitioners provide selected minor skin procedures when clinically appropriate. Whether a biopsy or removal is suitable depends on the lesion, the practitioner’s assessment and the procedure required.

How often should I have a skin check?

There is no single interval for everyone. Frequency depends on your personal risk factors, history and clinical findings. Your practitioner can recommend an appropriate follow-up plan.

What should I bring to a skin-check appointment?

Bring information about previous skin cancers, biopsies, pathology or specialist reports if you have them. If you have noticed a change over time, dates or photographs can also be helpful context.
