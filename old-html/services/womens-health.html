What is this service?

Healthcare that changes with each stage of life.

Women’s health in general practice includes preventive care, reproductive and sexual health, pregnancy-related care, menstrual concerns, menopause and broader physical and mental wellbeing.

A GP can provide assessment and ongoing care, discuss screening and treatment options, and arrange specialist referral when this is clinically appropriate.

The practice also has related specialist gynaecology care represented within its practitioner directory.

Why does it matter?

Health priorities can change over time.

Preventive screening, contraception, pregnancy planning, menopause and other health concerns can become relevant at different times. Regular care helps these issues be considered in the context of your medical history, medicines, family history and preferences.

Early discussion can also help when symptoms are new, persistent or affecting daily life.

How can we help?

Support across preventive, reproductive and life-stage care.

Depending on your needs and the practitioner you see, care may include:

  • Preventive health and screening discussions
  • Contraception and reproductive health discussions
  • Pregnancy planning and pre-pregnancy health
  • Shared antenatal or pregnancy-related care where appropriate
  • Menstrual or hormonal health concerns
  • Menopause and healthy ageing discussions
  • Mental health and general wellbeing
  • Referral for specialist gynaecology or other care when clinically appropriate

Understanding your care

Screening and treatment are individual.

The screening or follow-up that is right for one person may not be right for another. Age, symptoms, screening history, pregnancy status, family history and other risk factors can all change what is appropriate.

If you are attending for several issues, pregnancy planning, menopause, a procedure discussion or a complex history, ask reception whether a longer appointment would be helpful.

This information is general in nature. Your practitioner can provide advice based on your individual health history and circumstances.

What should I do next?

Start with a confidential conversation about what you need.

Book with a practitioner whose verified interests match your needs, or ask reception to help identify an appropriate appointment type.

  • Book a GP appointment for preventive care, reproductive health or new symptoms.
  • Ask about a longer appointment if you have several concerns or a complex history.
  • Bring relevant screening history, medication information and recent reports if available.
  • Seek urgent medical care for severe or rapidly worsening symptoms; call 000 for a life-threatening emergency.

Planning your appointment

Booking, preparation, follow-up and costs.

How to book

Book a GP appointment. Ask for a longer appointment if you want to discuss several issues, a procedure, pregnancy planning or a complex history.

Before your appointment

Bring relevant screening history, previous reports, pregnancy information and a current medicines list if these are not already available to the practice.

After your appointment & follow-up

Follow-up may include results, repeat screening, contraception review, pregnancy care, referral or a planned review appointment depending on your needs.

Costs

Standard Medicare-eligible GP consultations are 100% bulk billed at Woolloongabba Medical Centre for patients with a valid Medicare card. Procedures, consumables, medicines or devices, reports and other non-Medicare services may attract additional fees. Reception will confirm the current practice charge before treatment or booking wherever practical. External providers set and bill their own fees.

Patient questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should cervical screening be performed?

Screening recommendations depend on your age, screening history and individual circumstances. Your doctor can confirm the interval that applies to you under current Australian guidance.

Do you provide menopause support?

Yes. Menopause-related symptoms, health considerations and treatment options can be discussed with your doctor.

Can I discuss contraception or pregnancy planning with a GP?

Yes. General practitioners can discuss contraceptive options, pregnancy planning and pre-pregnancy health, and arrange referral when specialist input is appropriate.

Can I request a female practitioner?

Practitioner availability varies. Reception can help you identify available female practitioners when you book.
