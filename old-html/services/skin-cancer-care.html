What is this service?

Skin cancer care begins with careful assessment and a clear next-step plan.

Skin cancer care in general practice can include assessment of suspicious lesions, discussion of risk and appropriate follow-up when a spot needs more than routine observation.

Depending on the clinical findings and the practitioner providing your care, the next step may include monitoring, a minor procedure such as biopsy or excision, pathology review or referral for specialist assessment.

Why does it matter?

Early assessment helps patients act on concerning skin changes.

Many skin lesions are benign, but some need prompt investigation. A new or changing lesion, or one that bleeds, crusts, changes colour or shape, or otherwise concerns you, should be assessed rather than ignored.

Your individual risk can also be influenced by previous skin cancer, sun exposure, skin type, family history and previous pathology.

How can we help?

Assessment, procedures, pathology follow-up and referral where appropriate.

Care may include:

  • Clinical assessment of suspicious or changing lesions
  • Review of personal and family skin-cancer history
  • Discussion of monitoring and follow-up intervals
  • Selected minor procedures, including biopsy or excision, where clinically appropriate and available
  • Review and explanation of pathology results
  • Planning further treatment when a diagnosis requires it
  • Referral for specialist assessment or treatment when needed
  • Preventive advice about sun exposure and monitoring your skin

Understanding your care

A diagnosis may involve more than one step.

Assessment does not always provide a final diagnosis immediately. If tissue is sent to pathology, your practitioner will explain how results will be followed up and what happens next.

If you have previous pathology, specialist letters or photographs showing how a lesion has changed, bring them to your appointment when available.

This information is general in nature. Your practitioner can provide advice based on your individual history and examination.

What should I do next?

Book an assessment if you have a lesion that concerns you.

When booking, tell reception if the appointment is for a particular lesion, a broader skin review, or follow-up after a previous biopsy or procedure. A longer appointment may be appropriate for a full skin check or more complex concerns.

If a procedure is recommended, it may need to be booked separately. Fees may apply to procedures and other non-Medicare services; contact reception for pricing details.

Planning your appointment

Booking, preparation, follow-up and costs.

How to book

Book a GP appointment for assessment of a suspicious or changing skin lesion. A separate procedure appointment may be needed for biopsy or excision.

Before your appointment

Avoid covering the lesion with heavy makeup or products on the day if practical. Bring relevant previous skin pathology or specialist information if available.

After your appointment & follow-up

Follow-up depends on the assessment and any pathology result. The practice will explain wound care, result communication, surveillance or referral when needed.

Costs

Standard Medicare-eligible GP consultations are 100% bulk billed at Woolloongabba Medical Centre for patients with a valid Medicare card. Procedures, consumables, medicines or devices, reports and other non-Medicare services may attract additional fees. Reception will confirm the current practice charge before treatment or booking wherever practical. External providers set and bill their own fees.

Patient questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a skin check and skin cancer care?

A skin check is the assessment step. Skin cancer care may also include planning further investigation, a minor procedure, pathology follow-up or referral when a lesion needs additional assessment or treatment.

Can a GP remove or biopsy a suspicious lesion?

Some practitioners provide selected minor skin procedures when clinically appropriate. Whether a biopsy or removal is suitable depends on the lesion, the practitioner’s assessment and the procedure required.

What happens if pathology shows a skin cancer?

Your practitioner will discuss the result and the appropriate next step. Depending on the diagnosis and clinical circumstances, this may include further treatment at the practice or referral for specialist care.

Should I wait for a routine skin check if a spot is changing?

A new, changing, bleeding, crusting or otherwise concerning lesion should be assessed rather than waiting for a routine screening interval.

Do fees apply to skin procedures?

Fees may apply to procedures and other services that are not covered as a standard Medicare-eligible general practice consultation. Contact reception before the appointment if you would like pricing information.
