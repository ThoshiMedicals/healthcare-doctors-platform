Procedure information

What this care involves.

Nebulised treatment may be used in selected clinical situations when a clinician considers it appropriate. For many people with asthma, a puffer with a spacer can be as effective as a nebuliser. Severe breathing difficulty is an emergency and should not wait for a routine appointment.

What happens during the appointment?

If nebulised treatment is indicated, the medicine is placed in the nebuliser and inhaled through a mask or mouthpiece while you are monitored. The clinician may reassess breathing, oxygen levels or symptoms afterwards and decide whether further treatment is needed.

This page provides general information only. The treating clinician will explain what is appropriate for your individual circumstances and obtain consent where a procedure is planned.

Before your appointment

How to book and prepare.

How to book

If you are seeking review for breathing symptoms, book a GP appointment or call reception. Nebulising is provided only when clinically appropriate after assessment; it is not booked as a routine treatment without clinical review.

Before your appointment

Bring your inhalers, spacer and current medicines list if available. Tell the clinician about your diagnosis, recent symptoms and what medicines you have already used. If breathing is severe, worsening rapidly or making it difficult to speak, walk or stay awake, seek emergency care.

After your appointment

Follow-up and aftercare.

The clinician will advise whether you can return home, need medication changes, require follow-up, or need urgent hospital assessment. Continue your asthma or respiratory action plan as directed.

Costs

Patient questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I book a nebuliser treatment without seeing a doctor?

Nebulising is used when clinically appropriate after assessment. Book for review of the breathing problem rather than assuming a nebuliser is the required treatment.

Is a nebuliser always better than a puffer?

No. Asthma Australia notes that for many people a reliever puffer used with a spacer is as effective as, or better than, a nebuliser.

What if breathing is very difficult?

Call Triple Zero (000) for severe or life-threatening breathing difficulty rather than waiting for a routine practice appointment.

Will I need follow-up after treatment?

That depends on the cause and severity of symptoms. The clinician may adjust medicines, provide an action plan or arrange further review.
