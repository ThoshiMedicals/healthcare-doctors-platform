What is this service?

Healthcare before a problem becomes urgent.

Preventive healthcare focuses on reducing health risks, identifying concerns early and keeping recommended care up to date before symptoms become serious.

In general practice, prevention may include age- and risk-appropriate screening discussions, immunisation review, lifestyle support, family-history assessment and monitoring of important health risks.

Preventive care is not a fixed checklist. What is appropriate depends on your age, sex, medical history, family history, medicines and individual risk factors.

Why does it matter?

Prevention creates opportunities for earlier action.

Some conditions develop gradually and may have few symptoms at first. Regular preventive care gives you and your GP an opportunity to review risk, discuss screening and identify changes that may benefit from follow-up.

It also helps keep vaccinations, chronic disease risk and lifestyle goals connected to your broader health plan.

How can we help?

Risk review, screening and prevention.

Preventive healthcare may include:

  • General health and risk-factor review
  • Age- and risk-appropriate screening discussions
  • Blood pressure and other clinically appropriate health checks
  • Vaccination and immunisation review
  • Lifestyle, nutrition, physical activity and smoking discussions
  • Family-history and cardiovascular risk review
  • Women’s, men’s and children’s preventive health discussions
  • Follow-up or referral when a risk or abnormal result needs further assessment

Understanding your care

Screening should be appropriate for you.

More testing is not always better. Screening has benefits and limitations, and the right approach depends on your individual risk and current Australian recommendations.

Your GP can explain why a particular check is or is not recommended and what should happen after a result.

This information is general in nature. Your practitioner can provide advice based on your individual health history and circumstances.

What should I do next?

Book a preventive health discussion based on your own risks and history.

If it has been some time since your last general health review, or if your family history or health risks have changed, book a preventive health discussion with a GP.

  • Bring an up-to-date medication list and relevant family-health information.
  • Tell your GP about changes in smoking, alcohol, exercise, weight or other health risks where relevant.
  • Ask which vaccinations and screening programs are appropriate for you.
  • Book follow-up if a result or risk factor needs further assessment.

Planning your appointment

Booking, preparation, follow-up and costs.

How to book

Book a GP appointment for preventive care. A longer appointment may help if several screening, lifestyle or risk-factor topics need review.

Before your appointment

Bring relevant screening history, vaccination records, family history and a current medicines list if available.

After your appointment & follow-up

Your GP will explain which screening or monitoring is appropriate, when it is due and how results will be followed up.

Costs

Standard Medicare-eligible GP consultations are 100% bulk billed at Woolloongabba Medical Centre for patients with a valid Medicare card. Procedures, consumables, medicines or devices, reports and other non-Medicare services may attract additional fees. Reception will confirm the current practice charge before treatment or booking wherever practical. External providers set and bill their own fees.

Patient questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What happens during a preventive health review?

A review may include discussion of your history, family history, lifestyle, medications and relevant screening or risk assessments. The content is tailored to the individual.

Do all adults need the same screening tests?

No. Screening recommendations vary according to age, history and risk. Your GP can explain what is appropriate for you.

Can vaccination status be reviewed during a GP visit?

Yes. Your doctor can review relevant immunisation needs and advise on next steps based on current recommendations and your circumstances.

Can preventive care be discussed even if I feel well?

Yes. Preventive healthcare is specifically intended to identify risks and opportunities before problems necessarily cause symptoms.
