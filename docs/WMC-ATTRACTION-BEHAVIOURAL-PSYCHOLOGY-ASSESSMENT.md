# GP Attraction — Behavioural Psychology Assessment

**Lens:** Behavioural psychology · healthcare decision science · employer-brand psychology · conversion psychology  
**Out of scope:** UX, typography, spacing, design system, SEO, navigation quality  
**Journey (frozen):** Join → Practice Experience → Community & Catchment → Continuity → Clinical Interests → GP Opportunities → Readiness (optional) → Opportunity Pack → Express Interest  

**Assumed visitor:** Never heard of WMC · comparing practices · time-poor · mildly skeptical · risk-sensitive · may be interstate / OTG / part-time / established  

Interactive maps: canvas `attraction-behavioural-psychology-assessment`

---

## 1. Executive psychological assessment

A cold GP does not arrive seeking “content.” They arrive seeking **risk reduction**: Will this place waste my time, misrepresent itself, or trap me in the wrong model of practice?

The Question-Led Journey is psychologically well-ordered: it answers *fit before ask*, which matches how risk-sensitive clinicians decide. Curiosity opens at Join; the deepest attention and trust gains sit in **Practice Experience** and **Catchment**; **Opportunities** is the fit-commitment hinge; **EOI** is a low-stakes social ask (“introduce yourself”) — good for hesitant actors.

Where the psychology fails authenticity is not in the question sequence but in **proof**. Concept imagery and absent real voices leave the GP in “I understand the story” without reaching “I could work *here*.” Optional readiness tools reintroduce **evaluation anxiety** (scoring the self) at the worst moment — after fit interest but before contact. Opportunity Pack risks a **closure illusion** (“I saved the pages; I’ll come back”) that substitutes for EOI.

**Stakeholder-ready score: 7.5/10** — Safe to show stakeholders the decision narrative; honesty cues and journey logic are strong.  
**Production-ready score: 5.5/10** — Not yet strong enough for a skeptical comparator GP to EOI without real WMC evidence.

## 2. Attention

### Moments of attention (high)

| Moment | Why attention spikes |
| --- | --- |
| Join eyebrow: “Could this practice be a fit?” | Self-relevant question; opens approach motivation |
| Join → Recommended Next: “What is a consulting week like?” | Concrete, professional curiosity; reduces abstract “careers page” threat |
| PE H1 + Typical Day / Nursing / honesty cues | Diagnostic of model-of-care; high information value for risk-sensitive GPs |
| Catchment: “Who will I be caring for?” | Identity + case-mix; interstate/OTG especially attentive |
| Opportunities: “Would I fit here?” + vacancy cards | Commitment-relevant; compares to other offers |
| EOI: “Introduce yourself” / “short introduction is enough” | Lowers social cost of first contact |

### Moments attention is lost (drop)

| Moment | Why attention falls |
| --- | --- |
| Mid-PE after 3–4 chapters (~2000+ words on path) | Satiety; time-poor scanning replaces reading |
| Continuity → Clinical Interests back-to-back | Overlapping “would I fit” themes; diminishing returns |
| Readiness numeric ratings (0–30) | Task-switch from “explore practice” to “assess myself” |
| Opportunity Pack as bookmark list | Archival behaviour; attention leaves the live decision |
| Concept faces / non-local rooms (if detected) | Authenticity alarm → defensive skim |

## 3. Trust map

### Trust increases
- Honesty cues (“may not suit throughput”; “no full book day one”; nursing cover constraints)  
- Operational specificity (handoffs, recalls, session constraints)  
- Place specificity (Vulture Street, suburbs, hospitals as context not boast)  
- Optional framing on tools (“not a gate”)  
- EOI as conversation, not application theatre  

### Trust decreases
- Imagery that does not feel like *this* practice  
- Silence where “GP voices” should be (suppressed composites = empty proof)  
- Product residue / framework titles in meta periphery  
- Vacancy commercial detail without relational proof  
- Self-scoring tools that feel like HR gating despite copy saying otherwise  

### Language that increases trust
- “Optional · not a gate”  
- “Honesty cue” + concrete constraint  
- “Introduce yourself” / “short introduction is enough”  
- “Part-time here is a reliability agreement…”  
- “Who will I be caring for?”  

### Language that creates doubt
- Anything that smells like assessment, score, readiness percentage  
- Over-smooth culture claims without named humans  
- “Framework / Network” product labels (even in titles)  
- Guarantees or implied volume promises

## 4. Cognitive load

| Page | Load type | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Join | Low–moderate | Orientation; many links but question scaffolding helps |
| Practice Experience | **High** | Seven chapters + honesty; valuable but long for one sitting |
| Catchment | Moderate | Manageable if PE already read; new schema (place/patients) |
| Continuity | Moderate–high after PE | Partial redundancy with PE Continuity chapter |
| Clinical Interests | Moderate–high | Another fit schema; decision fatigue if PE+Catchment+Continuity already done |
| Opportunities | Moderate | Fit + vacancies = two tasks; still necessary |
| Readiness | **High (wrong kind)** | Multi-attribute numeric self-evaluation → decision fatigue |
| Opportunity Pack | Low | But encourages deferral |
| EOI | Low | Good — if momentum survives |

**Unnecessary decisions:** whether to “score” readiness; how many optional tools to open; whether to “save pages” instead of writing.

## 7. Conversion map

| Surface | Moves toward action? | Slows momentum? | Helping? | Unnecessary? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Practice Experience** | Yes (primary) | Only via length | **Core helper** | No — but completable in one pass ideally |
| **Community** | Yes (place/identity) | Mild if long | Strong helper | No |
| **Opportunities** | Yes (hinge) | If commercial without relational proof | Essential | No |
| **Readiness** | Rarely | **Yes — strongly** | Only for anxious planners | **Psychologically optional; often harmful to momentum** |
| **Opportunity Pack** | Weak | **Yes — deferral** | Mild for true later returners | Often unnecessary on first visit |
| **Express Interest** | Yes (if reached) | Only if form feels “application” | Essential endpoint | No |

**Continuity + Clinical Interests** (between Catchment and Opportunities): help *some* GPs (interest-led, continuity-anxious) but for time-poor comparators they are **secondary**; psychologically they can dilute peak PE→Opp momentum.

## 8. Trust builders (ranked)

1. **Honesty / constraint disclosure** (strongest for skeptical clinicians)  
2. **Continuity as operations** (not slogan)  
3. **Professional specificity** (handoffs, sessions, cover)  
4. **Support visibility** (nursing/reception as real)  
5. **Belonging cues** (people, voices, “fit” without coercion)  
6. **Certainty of next step** (EOI low-stakes + who reads it)  
7. **Place certainty** (catchment)  
8. **Commercial clarity** (vacancies as secondary after fit)

## 9. EOI hesitation analysis (barriers)

| Barrier | Mechanism |
| --- | --- |
| Uncertainty of authenticity | “Is this really WMC?” (imagery/voices) |
| Missing proof of people | No named/consented humans → abstract practice |
| Fear of wrong fit | High for established / part-time / OTG; needs stronger honesty + examples |
| Interstate / relocation unknown | Logistics under-served psychologically after Catchment |
| OTG / paperwork anxiety | Tools exist but feel like gates; or skipped → residual fear |
| Fear of change / status loss | Leaving current practice; site can’t fully solve — EOI must feel reversible |
| Comparison incomplete | Still shopping; Pack enables delay |
| Social risk of contacting | Who reads EOI? What happens next? Timeline? |
| Vacancy mismatch | “No role / wrong role” → approach avoidance |
| Evaluation anxiety | Readiness scores → “I’m not ready enough to write” |

## 10. Belonging analysis

| State | Meaning | Current platform |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **“I understand this place.”** | Cognitive map of model, patients, continuity | **Largely achieved** via PE + Catchment + honesty |
| **“I could work here.”** | Self–place fit + social proof + reversible next step | **Partially achieved** — language yes; proof weak; optional tools undercut |

Difference: understanding is **comprehension**; belonging is **identity + safety to approach**.  
WMC currently sells comprehension well and belonging incompletely.

## 11. Patient → Book appointment (adaptation only)

Same psychology, different stakes:

| Principle | GP Attraction | Patient booking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attention | Fit questions | Symptom / access questions (“Do I need to be seen?”) |
| Trust | Honesty about model | Honesty about wait, fees, who they’ll see |
| Load | Don’t force self-scoring before contact | Don’t force account/tooling before book |
| Emotion | Belonging → EOI | Safety/relief → Book |
| Friction | Pack deferral | “Call later” / multi-step forms |
| Conversion | EOI as introduce | Book as concrete slot or clear phone fallback |
| Belonging | “I could work here” | “I’ll be looked after here” |

Do not redesign; when patient journeys are addressed, preserve **low-stakes ask**, **honesty under uncertainty**, and **avoid assessment theatre before action**.

## 12. Top 10 psychological improvements

1. Replace concept imagery with recognisable WMC proof (authenticity alarm off).  
2. Add 3–6 consented voices (social proof for belonging).  
3. State who reads EOI and what happens next (certainty → action).  
4. Keep Readiness off the default path (remove evaluation anxiety).  
5. Treat Opportunity Pack as post-EOI or explicit “return later,” not pre-EOI.  
6. Shorten perceived PE load (chapter completable; honesty first).  
7. Soften Continuity→Interests as optional depth for time-poor path.  
8. Strengthen interstate/OTG reassurance without new modules (honest lines in Catchment/FAQ/EOI).  
9. Keep vacancy cards subordinate to fit narrative (avoid commercial hijack).  
10. Preserve and amplify honesty cues — they are the #1 trust builder for this audience.

## Final answer

**As a psychologist, why would a GP submit an EOI — or fail to submit one?**

A GP submits when the site has reduced **career risk** enough that a short introduction feels safer than continued comparison: they believe the continuity model is real, the patient mix is clear, the fit ask is honest, and contacting the practice is **reversible and human** (“someone here will read this”).  

A GP fails to submit when understanding never converts to belonging — usually because proof of *this* practice is thin, optional tools make them feel assessed or “not ready,” or a pack/bookmark lets them postpone without resolving uncertainty about who they would join and what happens after they write.
